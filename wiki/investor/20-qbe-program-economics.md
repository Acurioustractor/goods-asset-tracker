# QBE Catalysing Impact: the program economics, and what $400K actually is

**Researched 2026-08-01 from primary sources.** Social Impact Hub's own pages and the QBE
Foundation announcement, not from our own documents. This file exists because every Goods
surface treats AU$400,000 as the plan, and the program's published numbers do not support that.

---

## The cohort

**A Curious Tractor is one of ten** in the 2026 Catalysing Impact cohort, announced
23 March 2026. The others: Adapt Homes, Envirotech Education, Good Cycles, Hepburn Energy,
Mycelia Renewables, Plate It Forward, Revolve Recycling, RoboFit, Ngardara Project.

Note that **Good Cycles was also in the 2025 cohort**, so repeat participation happens.

## The pool

| | |
|---|---|
| 2026 total pool | **up to AU$1.1 million in catalytic grants, across all ten** |
| 2025 actual | AU$1.02 million across ten enterprises |
| 2025 average | **~AU$102,000** |
| SIH's stated range | "typically AU$150,000 to AU$400,000" |

**So AU$400,000 is the top of the range and 36% of the entire pool.** Planning as though it
is the expected case is planning on the best outcome available to any enterprise in the room.

## Leverage is the metric they publish

2025: AU$1.02M of QBE grants leveraged **AU$2.75M of external funding**, for a total capital
stack of AU$3.77M. **3.7x.** Plus AU$54,995 pledged at a live crowdfunding showcase at QBE's
Sydney head office from the Foundation and QBE employees together.

If leverage is what the program measures and publicises, it is what an application should
argue. A large external stack against a modest ask is a stronger case than the reverse.

## "Catalytic", not "matching"

SIH describes the money as **"catalytic, helping you unlock additional investment"** and says
it works with **"external commitments you have secured"**. The recorded Stage 2 terms say the
grant **"must be at least matched by SIGNED external commitments"**.

Read carefully, that is a **coverage test on whatever QBE decides to give**, not a promise to
double what Goods raises. Raising AU$400,000 does not entitle Goods to AU$400,000.

⚠ **Our own locked line says otherwise.** `ASK_HEADLINE.line` in `ask-surface.ts`, locked by
Ben 2026-07-21, reads: *"We are raising $400K in signed commitments by 31 August. QBE matches
it dollar for dollar: an $800K program that takes Goods to the point it funds itself."* Both
halves of that are at risk: "matches it dollar for dollar" is our framing rather than the
program's, and "$800K program" assumes the maximum grant. At the year-one average the program
is AU$400-500K, not AU$800K.

## Eligibility, and the entity problem

SIH's page states applicants must be **"Australian-incorporated"** social enterprises, and
that **"all participating enterprises must be seeking investment or repayable debt"**. It also
expects enterprises to be planning to raise capital **between March and August 2026**, which
is probably where our own 31 August gate came from.

The cohort entrant is **A Curious Tractor**, which is incorporated. But all trading and all
financial statements sit in **Nicholas Marchesi, sole trader**, which is not the applicant and
is not incorporated. `STRATEGY.md` records an "entity mismatch in the QBE agreement" that has
never been raised with SIH. This research turns that from a tidiness problem into the gap
between the applicant and its own accounts.

## What follows from this

1. **The repayable column is the main event, not the balance.** If QBE lands at AU$100-200K
   against a bottom-up need of AU$367-620K, then SEFA and the other lenders are the raise,
   not a top-up to a grant.
2. **Lead with leverage.** A large signed external stack against a modest ask argues QBE's own
   published metric back to them.
3. **The applicant is almost certainly A Curious Tractor Pty Ltd**, and it needs financial
   statements that describe it.
