# SEFA Backing the Bold

> Backing the Bold is a loan of $50,000 to $200,000 for impact-led organisations that already
> trade. The Queensland stream is open with rolling acceptance and no closing date, Goods on
> Country is Queensland based, and the expression of interest takes under fifteen minutes. It is
> drafted and unsent. Two required fields decide it before a word of narrative matters: four years
> of EBITDA, and the percentage of revenue that comes from trade. The Butterfly Movement Ltd shows
> an FY26 EBITDA of about negative $42,854 and the trading entity shows roughly $168,000, so the
> form makes Ben, Nic and Joel settle which entity applies. Jay's steer for QBE 2026 is that
> corporate philanthropy should unlock debt or equity, and the $600,000 asked across five lines is
> all philanthropy, which is why sending this beats any wording change anywhere.

## What this page replaces

The previous version of this profile asked SEFA for a working capital sub-debt tranche of
$300,000. Backing the Bold lends $50,000 to $200,000, so that ask sat $100,000 above the ceiling of
the product that is open to us. The programme has also been written up in places as a CIFA grant.
It is SEFA and it is a loan. The CFA Loan Fund, $200,000 to $2M, is a separate SEFA product and is
not the one in front of us.

The raise carries a Sefa line at $200,000 in the cash forecast. That figure is ours. SEFA has named
no number, no expression of interest has been sent, and `capital-stack-flex.ts:167-175` holds the
SEFA line at zero and outside the raise for that reason. It belongs under a subtotal labelled
proposed borrowing until an EOI exists.

## The two fields that decide it

The EOI asks what percentage of revenue comes from trade, and for EBITDA across FY26, FY25, FY24
and FY23. Joel flagged the entity question on 21 August as the thing to settle before approaching
investors. The form now asks it directly, and the answer changes completely with the entity.

| | The Butterfly Movement Ltd | The trading entity |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The charity. DGR endorsed. Applicant and recipient for every grant under ruling AA | Nicholas Marchesi as sole trader, moving to A Curious Tractor Pty Ltd |
| FY26 income | $77,080 | About $713,827, which may be cited and is never called signed |
| FY26 result | Expenses of $119,934, so EBITDA of about negative $42,854 | A net profit of roughly $168,000 before founder wages |
| Revenue from trade | Almost none. A gifted charity that has barely traded | Every bed invoice sits here |
| FY23 to FY25 | A dormant shell, close to zero | Where the history is |

Applying as Butterfly puts four years of a gifted shell in front of a lender, with a negative FY26
at the end of it. That is not a lending case. Applying as the trading entity gives SEFA something
to lend against and opens the question of which entity repays, because the bed revenue sits with
the sole trader and the grants sit with the charity.

This is a conversation between Ben, Nic and Joel before it is a writing task. Joel offered a call
with Tanya from SEFA's investment team on 21 August to look at the financials together. That call
was not taken, and it is the fastest way to settle a field the form requires.

### The three answers that make the case

The form asks for up to three biggest challenges. The honest three are accessing capital or
financing, lack of capacity or resources, and governance structures. The third one is
uncomfortable and true: the constitution is unlocated, there are no board sub-committees, no
skills matrix and no risk framework. Saying it is better than letting SEFA's investment team find
it in due diligence.

What the loan would enable: expand operations, and fund working capital.

Growth goal over two to three years: 400 beds of first stock in year one, 628 in year two and 900
in year three. 628 is the point at which trade carries the whole organisation with no grant
support, and it is provisional, because it derives from the provisional contribution below. Two
community plants in year one, with a third already approved for Alice Springs.

## What the loan should be sized to

| | Amount | Why debt suits it |
|---|---|---|
| A second tab press | $22,500 | A machine with a measurable output. It lifts modelled dispatch toward the router ceiling |
| Working capital for a production run | $276 a bed, provisional | Beds are bought before they are made, and the money comes back on the invoice when the bed sells at the published $750 |
| A sensible first ask | $50,000 to $100,000 | Inside the programme band, and inside one plant's modelled output to repay |

Both unit figures are provisional. Canon labels the $276 a legacy making allowance and labels the
$474 a provisional contribution, because Ben's 12 September route ruling buys leg sheets and nobody
has cut and counted leg kits from a purchased panel yet. Nic owns that yield, from the cutting
evidence on the 25 sheets already invoiced.

At the provisional $474 a bed, 101 paid beds a year services $200,000. That sits inside one plant's
modelled output and well inside what four organisations have already bought: 320 beds across five
settled invoices, $247,770 net. The repayment source is trade and the trade exists.

A grant funder will not buy a press. A lender will, because a press produces the cash that repays
it. Across the four applications open now that is the cleanest division of labour: QBE buys plants,
Tim Fairfax buys the organisation, Brian M. Davis buys beds and facilitation, and SEFA lends
against the machine and the stock.

The second press is cross-guarded across `defy-supply.ts`, `production-scenarios.ts` and
`the-year-and-the-raise.ts` at $22,500. Nobody has quoted it, and it appears in no ask.

## Why this outranks any wording change

Jay, recorded on the QBE final application page:

> Capital, whether that's debt or equity, will be more favourably looked at rather than
> philanthropy. In theory, they don't want to use their corporate philanthropy to unlock other
> corporate philanthropy... this year they really want to prove this concept that corporate
> philanthropy can be used to unlock impact investment... if the whole thing is philanthropy, that
> will be something the Steerco will have to discuss, because that's a big change this year.

The raise asks $600,000 across five lines and has $0 secured. QBE, Tim Fairfax, Brian M. Davis and
Snow are all grants. A SEFA letter of intent does not have to be a signed deal to change the shape
of that, and the QBE application closes Friday 25 September 2026 at noon. A fifteen-minute EOI is
the cheapest thing available to the QBE outcome.

## What the older record still gives us

Hannah Ebeling, SEFA's CEO, described the organisation's standard lending at the QBE induction: no
early repayment penalty, with more than 40% of clients repaying early; a 1% establishment fee
charged only after approval; an interest rate on capital and no other structure; and a willingness
to syndicate and bring other investors in, as SEFA did for Jigsaw. Her covenant description
included a majority of independent board directors or, at minimum, a structured advisory group,
ongoing impact and financial reporting, and an annual investment review.

That account describes SEFA's standard lending process and has not been checked against the
Backing the Bold terms. If the covenant holds here, it lands on the same governance gap the EOI
already asks us to declare.
