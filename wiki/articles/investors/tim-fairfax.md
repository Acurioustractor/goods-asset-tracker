# Tim Fairfax Family Foundation

> Katie Norman wrote to Nic and Ben on 31 August 2026 inviting an application for $300,000 over
> three years in three equal payments, and named the resilience of organisations as the reason.
> The real form arrived on 12 September and it is FY27 Resilience: Multi-Year General Operating
> Support, thirteen pages in SmartyGrants, closing Thursday 9 October 2026 at 5pm AEST with a board
> decision in late November. It tests governance maturity more than it tests the model. Fourteen
> required attachments, and we hold three of them: a budget, a profit and loss with a balance
> sheet, and photographic material. The constitution sits at field 4.1 and is unlocated, which is
> the same document that blocks two QBE questions. The form also requires a declaration of any AI
> use, and says plainly that no application is rejected for it. Year one is $100,000 and it belongs
> on the operating line alone.

The three-year case built for this funder is still the argument and it has one home on this form:
question 3.7, which carries a 200-word guide. Everything else on the thirteen pages is new work.

## The form tests governance more than it tests the model

The invitation names the resilience of organisations, and the form is built to test exactly that.
It asks for a strategic plan with KPIs, a board skills matrix, a risk framework, sub-committees,
appointment dates and a governance war story.

That is the honest weak point. Goods on Country has three First Nations directors, a constitution
nobody can find, no board sub-committees, no skills matrix and no risk framework. The financial
case is the strongest part of this application and the governance case is the thinnest. Two of the
missing documents are close to free, because TFFF supplies optional templates for the skills matrix
and the risk matrix, so 4.4 and 4.6 are an afternoon each.

## Field 4.1 and the constitution

4.1 is a required attachment and there is no text box or web link alternative on it. The
constitution is unlocated. It already blocks QBE Q12 and Q22, the 50% Indigenous business threshold
and IBA eligibility, and it now blocks a second funder. Eloise owns finding it.

The declaration at the end of the form raises the stakes on the same document. Point 3 asks the
signer to certify that the organisation's constitution or rules are consistent with ACNC and ATO
requirements for a charity, and that the organisation has acted consistently with its objects. A
person cannot honestly certify that about a document nobody has read.

## Four smaller fields that still need a person

**3.2** asks which description fits, and both boxes are ours to tick: First Nations governance and
executive leadership, and delivering services with First Nations communities. Three of three
directors are First Nations.

**3.3** asks for the Traditional Custodians and Country where the organisation is based, and for
the people and Country we support through the work. TFFF gives its own answer as the example:
Magandjin or Meeanjin, acknowledging the Yuggera and Turrbal people. Ours has to be written by
someone with the standing to write it, and checked with community before it goes on a form.

**3.4** asks for geographic reach from a checklist that covers the Northern Territory and
Queensland and then stops. Top End, Barkly region and Central Australia carry Maningrida, Tennant
Creek, Alice Springs and Utopia. North QLD carries Palm Island. Work in Kalgoorlie is in Western
Australia and the NPY lands cross three states, so both go under "Other" and should be named there
instead of dropped.

**5.5** asks which philanthropic funders currently fund us, from a checklist. FRRR and VFFF are
ticks, the latter for $50,000 under Backing the Future. Dusseldorp, Minderoo and Paul Ramsay are on
the list and are conversations, so they stay unticked. Hand Heart Pocket is also on that list and
co-runs SEFA's Backing the Bold, so the two applications touch.

## The AI-use declaration

The declaration page carries TFFF's Responsible AI Statement and asks organisations to declare any
use of AI tools in preparing the application. The form says it plainly: TFFF will not reject an
application or report because AI was used to prepare a submission. It also states that TFFF does
not use AI to review, assess or decide funding applications.

The options are a checklist. For this application the honest ticks are "Yes, to draft answers to
questions which were then edited by staff" and "Yes, to prepare/review documents uploaded as
attachments to this form". The three-year model, the capacity work and the financial modelling were
all built with AI assistance and then checked against invoices and the asset register. Say that in
the Other box in one sentence.

## The three years

Running the organisation costs $297,550 a year. A bed sold at $750 hands back a provisional $474
after a provisional making allowance, so a provisional 628 paid beds a year carries the whole
organisation with no grant support. Year one plans 400. The three years are the distance between
those two numbers.

| | Beds sold | Trade contributes | Trade covers | What the $100,000 does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| To June 2027 | 400 | $189,600 | 64% | Keeps the lights on. It is about a third of what the year costs |
| To June 2028 | 628 | $297,672 | 100% | Becomes the reserve that lets a bad quarter happen without a redundancy |
| To June 2029 | 900 | $426,600 | 143% | The handover, if year three pays for it: the legal work, the training and the asset transfer that move a plant Goods holds closer to community ownership |

Every figure in the middle three columns derives from the provisional $474, so every one of them is
provisional too. That is the argument for their money: the same $100,000 does a different job each
year because trade has grown underneath it. A grant that shrinks as a share of the budget is the
resilience Katie Norman named.

## Capacity, so no target is written that cannot be made

Under Ben's 12 September route ruling the current facility presses tab sheets only, buys leg
sheets, and dispatches flat-packed kits for young people to assemble in community. Factory capacity
ends at dispatch. That models 1,152 kits a year at six a day across sixteen run days a month. A new
plant makes about 200 beds in its first year and reaches 720 at maturity.

| | Plants running | Modelled capacity | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year one | 0 | 1,152 | 400 |
| Year two | 2 | 1,552 | 628 |
| Year three | 3 | 2,792 | 900 |

Every bed volume is a target that fits inside modelled capacity. None is a forecast, and no buyer
has ordered year two or year three.

## Four things nobody has ruled on

These belong in the application instead of under it, because a three-year budget that hides its
assumptions is worth less than one that names them.

1. Whether the running cost stays at $297,550 for three years while two plants and six communities
   are added, or grows.
2. Whether founder pay moves off $151,200 once trade carries the organisation.
3. Who carries freight from year two. With Goods carrying it the contribution drops to a provisional
   $324 and break-even moves from 628 beds to 918.
4. Whether year three pays for the handover, which is the only line here that is not an operating
   cost.

One line has to be kept out. Facilitation bills at $10,000 a community and costs about the same to
deliver, so counting it as income without its cost repeats the mistake that produced the overstated
year figure earlier this month.
