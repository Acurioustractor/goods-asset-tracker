# The blockers

> Ten things stand between this organisation and four submitted applications, and each one is a document somebody has to hand over or a decision somebody has to make. The accountant's letter comes first because the QBE final application page records it as the submission blocker and leaves the owner column beside it empty. Butterfly's constitution comes second because one unlocated file stops four things at once across two funders. The other eight have names on them: Ben, Nic, Eloise, Joel Bird, and the board that sits on 14 September. Each entry below says what the thing is, who holds it as the record has it, what it blocks by question number, and what it is worth where a figure exists. ## 1. The accountant's letter, with no owner and no date

The QBE final application page carries an attachment register: three artifacts ready, seven needing work, three that do not exist yet. The accountant's letter sits in that register as the submission blocker, and nobody's name sits beside it. Everything else on that page belongs to somebody.

Standard Ledger are the accountants, and they are documenting the FY26 carve-out from a founder's sole trader ledger into the applicant. QBE asks for the applicant's profit and loss, balance sheet and cashflow at Q20 and Q21, and for financial readiness at Q19. The letter is what makes those three answers stand up.

If it does not arrive, the form does not go in. Two plants at $150,000 each, so $300,000 of the $600,000 asked, goes with it. This is the single most dangerous line in the raise because an unassigned item has nobody to chase. A name against it, and a date against the name, is the first move.

## 2. Butterfly's constitution, unlocated

The Butterfly Movement Ltd is the applicant and the recipient under ruling AA, and its constitution has not been found. A Curious Tractor's constitution cannot stand in for it. Eloise holds this one.

Four things wait on that file:

- **QBE Q12**, why governance documents are unavailable. Q12 exists to explain a genuine retrieval gap, so an honest answer is available either way.
- **QBE Q22**, evidence that governance is focused on impact, which wants the constitution among its uploads.
- **Tim Fairfax 4.1**, a required field on the FY27 Resilience form, where the constitution is one of fourteen required attachments and we hold three.
- **The 50% threshold.** An Indigenous business is one with at least 50% Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander ownership. Indigenous Business Australia applies the same test, and for a company limited by guarantee it reads as membership. Membership sits in that constitution, so eligibility for a whole class of First Nations finance turns on it too.

The handoff calls it the highest-value unread document in the organisation. That is the right description. One file, two funders, and a finance pathway.

## 3. Which entity holds the trade revenue: ruled, 12 September

Ben ruled it. There is one entity. The Butterfly Movement Ltd holds the grants, the trade, the wages and the resources, and its name changes to Goods on Country.

The Backing the Bold EOI takes under fifteen minutes and asks two things: EBITDA for FY26, FY25, FY24 and FY23, and the percentage of revenue that comes from trade. Answered as Butterfly, that is an FY26 EBITDA of about negative $42,854, almost no trade revenue, and a dormant shell across FY23 to FY25. The trading entity would have shown roughly $168,000 before founder wages with all of its revenue from trade, and Ben ruled knowing that.

So the lending case is weaker as one entity, and the call carries this application. Joel Bird flagged exactly this on 21 August and offered a call with Tanya from the investment team. That call belongs before the EOI goes, and it is now the blocker in place of the entity question.

What the ruling moves onto the list instead: whether trading sits inside the charitable purposes and what routing every bed invoice through a DGR does to the endorsement, which is MinterEllison's; the ACNC and ASIC name change with the board resolution behind it, which can travel with the constitutional amendment blocker above; and documenting the transfer of trade from the sole trader, which is a related-party transaction.

Why this one outranks its size: Jay's steer for QBE 2026 is that corporate philanthropy should unlock debt or equity, and the $600,000 asked is 100% philanthropy. The EOI is drafted and unsent, the Queensland stream is open with rolling acceptance, and sending it does more for the QBE application than any wording change anywhere.

## 4. The site for the second Commonwealth $150,000: ruled, 12 September

Ben ruled it. The second $150,000 is all for Alice Springs, in line with the Oonchiumpa support. Both Commonwealth tranches land on the same site, so Alice Springs carries $300,000 across two tranches and builds no second plant.

Alice Springs is not one of the two sites in the QBE ask, so no QBE activity is funded twice and Q14 and Q15 carry no double-funding disclosure. Oonchiumpa's related-party disclosure stands on its own footing, because Kristy Bloomfield sits on both boards.

**The gap does not close.** Closing it needed the second $150,000 to land on a site QBE is already being asked to fund, which would have freed $150,000 of the QBE request to buy beds. On Alice Springs it is additional capacity instead, so the $147,950 and the 320 beds stand and have to come from beds sold or from debt. That puts more weight on the SEFA call.

## 5. Defy quotes QU0494 and QU0495, unopened

Ben or Nic. Two quotes from Defy Design sit unopened, and they carry the bag price for shred. That price decides the second press against buying panels.

The second press is $22,500. Nobody has quoted it and it sits in no ask, so nothing in the raise pays for it today. Defy's INV-2021 falls due on 18 September, the same day the shred runs out on Nic's own figure of 450 kg a week, so the money question and the supply question arrive together.

Under the route Ben ruled on 12 September, Witta presses tab sheets and the leg sheets are bought in, so what a second press adds is tab capacity. Opening two emails settles it.

## 6. Q19's six documents

Q19 is governance, team, financial and delivery readiness. Its text is written. Six documents stand behind it, and Nic holds most of them:

- A site agreement or letter of support for Palm Island. Nic.
- The same for Maningrida. Nic.
- A quote basis for each plant, against the thirteen modules. Nic.
- A cash milestone schedule tying the build to the funding dates. Nic and Eloise.
- Kristy Bloomfield's related-party declaration, minuted. The board, 14 September.
- Butterfly's constitution and current management cashflow for the applicant. Eloise.

Palm Island and Maningrida are working choices recorded as Ben's picks on 9 September. Neither community has agreed to a build, and neither should be told it is in a grant application before Ben has spoken with it. The site letters are a relationship step before they are a document.

## 7. Management cashflow and reconciled opening balances

Eloise. Q20 and Q21 ask for the applicant's profit and loss, balance sheet and cashflow, and a programme forecast will not answer them. The same documents reach Q24, the solvency declaration, where opening cash is recorded as unknown. Unknown is not the same as zero, and a solvency declaration signed on an unknown balance is a signature nobody should give.

Tim Fairfax wants the same class of document under its governance test, so this work pays twice.

## 8. Kristy Bloomfield's related-party minute

The board, sitting 14 September. Kristy is a director of The Butterfly Movement and of Oonchiumpa, and Oonchiumpa is the community partner commissioning a plant. She declares her interest at both boards and takes no part in either board's decision about a transaction between them. The minute is what puts that on the record.

It blocks Q8 and Q22, and Oonchiumpa is asked to minute the same thing from its side.

## 9. The child safety process

Ben. The policies register records the process at draft, with a consultation under way with Fred Campbell.

Brian M. Davis asks about the National Principles for Child Safe Organisations, and this programme puts young people on paid work. The honest answer names three things: the draft and its completion date, the community organisations whose own child safe policies and screening the work runs inside, and what upholding children's rights looks like in practice, which is that young people are paid at a named rate, their images and words are used only with recorded consent, and their community organisation decides what happens locally.

That question stays unsubmitted until the draft is finished or a date is set. A youth funder will read it closely, and overstating it would do more damage than naming the gap. The application is $100,000 and it is due 25 September.

## 10. Sharing the live workbook with the service account

Ben, and it is one share. Editor access to `subscription-scanner@act-subscription-tracker.iam.gserviceaccount.com`.

Until that happens, eleven drifted cells and four missing tabs stay as they are and the guarded modules cannot push canon into the workbook. The worst single cell is the Calculator's availability, still reading 100% where Ben set 80% on 10 September. That one cell drives three others, so every capacity figure downstream of it is wrong until it is set.

## Where this leaves the raise

$600,000 is asked across five lines. $0 is secured. The gap is $147,950.

None of the ten items above is a funder's decision. They are documents this organisation can produce, quotes it can open, a site it can name, a minute it can take and a file it can share. The one with no owner is the one to fix first.
