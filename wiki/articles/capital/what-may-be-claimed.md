# What may be claimed

> This is the claim ceiling in one place: the rules that govern every funder surface, every deck
> slide and every page a stranger can open. Each rule below carries the ruling that made it, the
> date, and one example of it being broken, because the broken version is usually good English and
> that is why it comes back. The pattern behind all of them is the same. Say what happened, name
> who did it and when, name the basis under every figure, and let the reader check. A claim that
> cannot survive a second question does more damage than the one nobody made.

## 1. There is no demand total, and none may be stated

The rule. Never print a total of beds wanted. Present acts, strongest first, and keep every bed
figure attached to the act that produced it.

The ruling. Ben, 11 September 2026: the figures are semantic, there is no real way to know, so the
question is how to talk about demand and who has asked.

The ladder, in order of strength.

1. Money moved. 320 beds bought and paid for by four organisations across five settled invoices,
   $273,966 including GST and $247,770 net.
2. Money named. Dianne Stokes, a Warumungu Elder at Tennant Creek, offered to self-fund twenty
   beds. She also named the Pakkimjalki Kari washing machines in Warumungu, so this is a long
   relationship.
3. An organisation asked. Utopia Homelands 150 beds with a rule of beds for every child, Homeland
   School Company 65 for the Maningrida homelands, Palm Island Community Company 40.
4. A person asked. Norman Frank asked for three beds in the maroon colourway, which is the smallest
   record and the most specific one on the list.
5. Raised in a meeting. Groote Archipelago 500, mentioned by Simone Grimmond at WHSAC with nothing
   following it. It is the largest figure and the weakest rung, and it may never appear without
   that status beside it.

Broken. The 778. It adds a figure scoped to one Elder's wallet to a figure scoped to the children
of an outstation to a figure nobody wrote a scope for at all. It is retired.

Broken again, in a way that looks like rigour. A rate of 0.26 beds per crowded dwelling is an
artefact: Maningrida's 65 is about the outstations and it was divided by the 2,518 people the ABS
counts in the whole Maningrida area. Anyone who builds on it will be wrong.

The one observation that is allowed. Palm Island reached one bed per 16.0 people and Tennant Creek
one per 15.9, measured on beds delivered, with no coordination between them. Both are records of
what two towns reached. Neither may be used to size a community nobody has counted.

## 2. Health is the reason and carries no number

The rule. The scabies to rheumatic heart disease chain is the reason, never a claimed outcome. No
Goods surface carries a health statistic as something Goods moved.

The ruling. The standing rule, carried on the deck's slide 14 as a ceiling Ben kept on 10
September 2026. It reads "The reason, not a claim".

Broken. A slide that prints a scabies rate beside a bed count. Neither figure is wrong and the
layout does the claiming, because a reader who sees them together reads cause. Nobody has measured
a health outcome from a Goods bed and nothing says otherwise.

## 3. Community ownership is a pathway

The rule. Ownership is always moving closer and never arrived. Community partners make the local
decisions today: how beds are used, who is paid and what is made next.

The ruling. Ruling X, Ben, 28 August 2026, which says in its own text that it does not turn the
pathway to community ownership into a completed claim.

Broken. Calling a plant community-owned while Goods still holds it. Zero community enterprises are
trading yet, which is what this year is for. Five communities have asked for a plant, and an ask is
an ask.

## 4. Revenue may be cited, and the basis travels with it

The rule. $713,827 is the Goods-only FY26 carve-out and may be cited. $741,111 is a different
basis, all-sources cash since inception, and may only appear when that basis is named in the same
sentence. $403,901 is never called a surplus. Nothing is ever called signed.

The ruling. Ruling G and ruling H, Ben, 25 July 2026: there is no signed accountant document, the
figure stays and the adjective goes. Ruling H supersedes the 24 July ruling that changed the figure
instead.

Broken. "Accountant-signed" ran on nine surfaces after ruling H was logged, six of them on the
funder-facing readiness page, one of them a green Verified chip. The ruling carried a sweep list
and the sweep had not happened. A ruling with a sweep list is not swept until something checks.

Still open. The accountant's letter is the recorded QBE submission blocker and it has no owner.

## 5. The trade record, with the basis named every time

The rule. Say what was invoiced, to whom, and whether the figure is gross or net. 320 beds bought
and paid for by four organisations across five settled invoices, $273,966 including GST and
$247,770 net, all of it in one Xero organisation, the sole trader Goods trades through. Facilitation
is a second thing buyers already pay for: $50,000 net across ten visits billed at $3,000, $6,000 and
$8,000. The published bed price is $750.

One live retail channel sits beside that record. Stripe sells the Stretch Bed, and one real outside
customer paid $750 on 8 September and is still waiting for the bed. That is one order, and it may
never be described as a retail line with traction.

Invoiced beds and deployed beds are two counts of two different things and are never added. 540
beds are deployed across eleven communities, a count that includes prototypes and gifted beds. 40
of those were made entirely at our own facility, pressed at Witta and assembled at Gamardi.

Broken. The $75,000 that sat on the deck against ALIVE was never on an invoice. ALIVE, the ALIVE
National Centre at the University of Melbourne, paid $101,200 including GST and $92,000 net on
INV-0342: 100 Stretch Beds at $800 plus four half-share workshops at $3,000. They are a paid
institutional buyer, and the deck both understated what they paid and read them as a research
relationship. INV-0341,
$66,000 including GST for a storytelling programme, is authorised and unpaid, due 30 July 2026, and
belongs in the same breath whenever receivables come up.

Broken again, by two numbers that match. Centrecorp paid for 167 beds, of which 147 are deployed
and 20 are made and waiting. Utopia's community total is also 147 and it is a different 147, since
Utopia holds eight beds from a batch Centrecorp never paid for. Neither is evidence for the other.
Centrecorp's beds are delivered and are never counted as demand.

## 6. The verified flag, and what false means

The rule. Public stats carry a flag of `verified: true` or `verified: false`. False means the claim
is stated and not yet evidenced: the 10+ year design life is design intent and renders without a
Verified badge. A flag is flipped to true when a source exists and never before.

The second half of the rule. `verified: false` is a legitimate state for a statistic and never a
state for words in a named person's mouth. A quote is real and traceable to the storyteller
registry, or it does not exist in the codebase.

Broken. `content.ts` once carried a `kristyQuote` whose words were invented and attributed by name
to Kristy Bloomfield, a Traditional Owner and Oonchiumpa director, marked `verified: false` with a
TODO. Nothing rendered it, so nothing caught it, and it sat shaped exactly like real quote data one
import away from a pitch page. `claims.guards.test.ts` now fails on any attributed quote that
cannot be traced.

## 7. A provisional figure says so in the sentence that prints it

The rule. Where canon marks a value provisional, the word goes in the sentence. A funder reading a
number cannot see a footnote nobody wrote.

The ruling. Ben, 12 September 2026, the flat-pack route: press the tab sheets only, buy the leg
sheets, dispatch flat-packed kits and run a programme where young people and others assemble and
distribute them in community. Canon's status line for the route cost reads in full:
"Provisional: bought legs and tab production need costing". The bought leg panel yield is unknown
until Nic counts it from the cutting evidence on the 25 sheets already invoiced.

What that touches. The making allowance and the contribution are both provisional, and break-even
at 628 beds a year derives from the provisional contribution, so 628 is provisional too. The $750
price is settled and unaffected.

Broken. Printing the contribution as a flat fact in a funder answer because the caveat lives in a
module comment. The caveat has to be in the sentence.

## 8. Designed in community

The rule. The products are designed in community, led by community. The word co-design is never
used on any surface, because it implies a facilitated joint process and the truth is that the design
happens in community.

The ruling. Ben, 11 July 2026.

Broken. Data keys like `theme: 'co-design'` still sitting in `content.ts` against real Tennant Creek
quotes, pending a separate migration. They are a record of the old vocabulary. No new ones get
added and none of them may surface as a label a reader sees.

## 9. One identity, and the entity facts under it

The rule. Goods on Country is the single operating and public identity, a registered business name
of The Butterfly Movement Ltd. There is no separate "Goods." maker or seller layer.

The ruling. Ruling X, Ben, 28 August 2026, superseding ruling K wherever it described "Goods." as
the current maker and seller. Ruling AA, Ben, 5 September 2026, holds the rest: Butterfly is
applicant and recipient, A Curious Tractor Pty Ltd is the trading entity, A Kind Tractor is dormant,
and DGR is only through Butterfly.

Broken. Reusing an archived deck or an old invoice as a description of how Goods works now. Those
are historical records and they stay historical.

## 10. The Weave Bed is discontinued, with one deliberate exception

The rule. Remove Weave Bed references from public surfaces. Supabase rows carrying
`product_type: weave_bed` or `weave-bed-*` slugs are wrong and belong as `stretch_bed`.

The exception, confirmed by Ben on 2 August 2026. Centrecorp's INV-0291 (107 beds, November 2025)
and QU-0014 (130 beds, May 2026) both read "Goods Weave Bed v2.3". Those are Stretch Beds. The line
name was held steady across the Centrecorp paper trail on purpose so their finance team sees the
same description invoice to invoice. Do not correct those documents.

Broken. Reading INV-0291 as evidence that a Weave Bed was ever produced at scale. Randle Walker
calls them Stretch beds in correspondence, which is the right name everywhere else.

## 11. Nothing is secured until somebody has decided it

The rule. $600,000 is asked across five lines and $0 is secured. An invitation is not an award, an
application is not a grant, and a conversation is neither.

Broken. The workbook's December receipts tab reads $700,000, a total that includes a SEFA line
nobody at SEFA has seen. The SEFA Backing the Bold EOI is drafted and unsent.

## How a figure gets into an article here

Settled figures are cited by canon key in the frontmatter, so when a module moves the article fails
by name. Everything else names its module and the date it was read, in `sources`. A figure with
neither is not ready to be printed, and the honest move is to name the act instead of the quantity.
