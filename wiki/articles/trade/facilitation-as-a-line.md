# Facilitation as a line

> Buyers already pay for two things. They pay for beds, and they pay for the time it takes to build them with a community. Across the five settled invoices, $50,000 net of community-build and program-support time was billed and paid alongside the 320 beds, in ten visits priced at $3,000, $6,000 and $8,000. That is a fifth of all settled net trade. It is the evidence under the $40,000 facilitation line in the raise, $10,000 for each of four communities, which the QBE application names at Q6, Q9 and Q19 as work its grant does not pay for and which is asked of the Brian M. Davis Foundation. A rate a buyer has already paid is a different kind of number from a rate a grant writer has estimated.

## The ten visits

| Invoice | Buyer | Place | Facilitation | What it was |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INV-0259 | Centrecorp Foundation | Utopia Homelands | $12,000 | Two bed-building workshops at $6,000 each |
| INV-0283 | Mala'la Health Service Aboriginal Corporation | Maningrida | $0 | None billed |
| INV-0291 | Centrecorp Foundation | Utopia Homelands | $18,000 | Three bed-building workshops at $6,000 each, same scope |
| INV-0303 | Homeland School Company | Maningrida homelands | $8,000 | One program-support trip to train, build, test, document and make with community |
| INV-0342 | ALIVE National Centre, University of Melbourne | Communities under Gathering the Parts | $12,000 | Four half-shares of a bed-building workshop at $3,000 each |

Ten visits. $50,000 net. Every line settled in full, the last of them on 20 August 2026.

## What a visit covers

The Centrecorp workshops, at $6,000, covered the community visit, working with Elders and young people on how the bed gets built locally, team time on the ground, transport and accommodation. That is the whole scope of the line as it was invoiced, and it is the same scope on both Centrecorp invoices.

The Homeland School Company trip, at $8,000, was one Goods on Country program-support trip to train, build, test, document and make with community, including travel and accommodation. It is the most expensive single visit anyone has paid for, and the 40 beds on that invoice are the 40 pressed at Witta and assembled at Gamardi with young people.

The ALIVE visits, at $3,000, are half-shares. ALIVE carries half the cost of each bed-building workshop and Goods carries the other half. The rate is not a discount on the $6,000 workshop. It is the same workshop split down the middle.

Mala'la paid none. Their invoice is 13 beds and $4,940 net, the smallest of the five, and it is the one where shipping was quoted at $3,200 and discounted in full.

## Why this is the strongest evidence in the raise

Four reasons, in the order a funder will test them.

**It is trade.** These are settled invoices in one Xero organisation, raised and paid. They are not letters of support, not pledges and not a pipeline. Anyone can check them by invoice number.

**The buyer set the price.** Three different organisations, across eleven months of invoices, chose to pay separately for the time. Centrecorp paid the same $6,000 rate on two invoices three months apart. ALIVE negotiated a half-share and still paid. A rate that survives a second purchase order at the same number has been tested.

**It is a fifth of all settled net trade.** $50,000 of $247,770 net is just over 20 per cent of everything the trade record holds. Facilitation is not a small add-on at the bottom of the bed line.

**It prices the ask honestly.** The raise asks $10,000 a community across four communities, $40,000 in all. The most expensive visit a buyer has ever paid for is $8,000, and the standard workshop rate is $6,000. So the ask sits a little above one billed visit, which is a defensible place to sit and an easy one to explain.

## What this money is not

It does not buy youth wages. In Alice Springs those are the Commonwealth's, through Oonchiumpa, and they sit outside this raise. It does not buy brokerage. The $60,000 youth-programme archetype that used to carry this line is retired.

It is also not proof that a funder will pay for facilitation. It is proof that buyers have. Those are two different claims and only the second one is evidenced.

## The word we do not use

These visits are how a bed gets designed and built in community, led by community. Never call them co-design. Co-design implies a facilitated joint process run from outside, and what happens on these trips is that the design happens in community, with Elders and young people deciding how the thing gets made in their place.

Community ownership stays a pathway throughout. A paid workshop moves a community closer to running the making itself. It does not complete that move and must never be written as though it has.

## Still open

Nic checks the overlap between the facilitation line and founder time and travel already sitting in the running cost. Travel to eleven communities carries $51,000 of the running cost on its own, so the two lines have to be read together before either is quoted to a funder.
