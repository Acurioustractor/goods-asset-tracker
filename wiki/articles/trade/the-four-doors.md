# The four doors

> Five settled invoices carry four kinds of buyer, and each kind has exactly one organisation behind it: an Aboriginal charitable trust, a university research centre, a homelands school company, and a community-controlled health service. Each door has a question that qualifies a buyer into it, one proof invoice, and the next organisation that has already asked in its own words. The record holds no owner for any of the four follow-ups. The grouping below is a reading of five invoices and is not a typed module, so check it against `demand-and-buyers.ts` before printing it anywhere.

## What this page is, and what it is not yet

`v2/src/lib/data/demand-and-buyers.ts` holds invoices. `v2/src/lib/data/who-has-asked.ts` holds acts on a ladder. Neither module holds a buyer type, and no guard tests the four doors below. Somebody reading five invoices can see four kinds of organisation in them, and that reading is useful enough to write down and weak enough to say so. Treat it as a working grouping until it is typed and guarded.

Two rules travel with every door.

- Money moved is the only rung nobody can argue with. Four organisations have paid for 320 beds, $273,966 including GST and $247,770 net. It is also the only rung that proves the price.
- There is no demand total and none may be stated. The 778 that used to be quoted added a figure scoped to one Elder's wallet to a figure scoped to the children of an outstation to a figure with no scope written for it at all. Adding them produces a number that is not a quantity of anything. Present acts, strongest first, with the bed figure attached to the act that produced it.

The ladder, from `who-has-asked.ts`: money moved · money named · an organisation asked · a person asked · it came up in a meeting.

## Door one · An Aboriginal charitable trust

**The qualifying question.** Does this body hold money for a named community, and can it decide to spend that money on beds without asking anyone else first?

**The proof invoice.** Centrecorp Foundation, INV-0259 and INV-0291. 167 beds paid for, $123,332 including GST across the two, delivered to Utopia Homelands. 147 are in households and 20 are made and waiting at Alice Springs. Centrecorp is the longest paying relationship on the record and the only buyer that came back for a second order.

**The next organisation that has asked.** None has asked through this door. The nearest open ask sits behind the same place: Utopia Homelands asked for 150 beds with a rule in their own words, beds for every child. That is an organisation asking, with no money named and nobody recorded as having said it. The child population of the homelands has never been stated, so the 150 is an impression with a rule behind it.

**Who owns the follow-up.** No owner recorded.

**Do not.** Centrecorp's beds are delivered and are never counted as demand (Ben, 11 September 2026). Quote QU-0014 for 130 beds was never paid. See [[trade/what-has-been-bought]].

## Door two · A university or research centre with its own programme budget

**The qualifying question.** Does this body control a programme budget, and does the bed already sit inside a programme it is running?

**The proof invoice.** ALIVE National Centre, University of Melbourne, INV-0342. 100 Stretch Beds at $800, $101,200 including GST and $92,000 net, paid in full on 20 August 2026 before a single bed was made. Beds go to communities under the Gathering the Parts programme. This is the largest bed order settled to date and the only one above list price.

**The next organisation that has asked.** None has asked through this door. The open item with ALIVE is INV-0341, $66,000 including GST for a twelve-month storytelling programme, authorised and unpaid past its 30 July 2026 due date. That is a collection and no part of it is a new ask.

**Who owns the follow-up.** No owner recorded.

**Say it properly.** ALIVE is a paid institutional buyer as well as a research relationship. A research centre paying up front for a hundred beds is the strongest single answer to a funder asking whether anyone will take these beds.

## Door three · A homelands school company

**The qualifying question.** Does this body run the places children sleep, and does it already buy things for those children?

**The proof invoice.** Homeland School Company, INV-0303. 40 Stretch Beds at $750, the first invoice at list price, with a washer, facilitation and freight on the same document, $44,000 including GST. These are the 40 beds pressed at Witta and assembled at Gamardi by local workers, so this door also carries the proof that the making path works.

**The next organisation that has asked.** Homeland Schools Co. asked for 65 beds, with a rule in its own words: beds for kids in the Maningrida homelands. It sits on the organisation-asked rung in `who-has-asked.ts`, not the raised-in-a-meeting rung, even though the ask was made in a meeting. The homelands are outstations and a fraction of the Maningrida area, so the 65 is scoped to a population nobody has counted. This is the only ask on the record whose counterparty has already paid Goods for beds, which is a payment history and says nothing about a second order.

**Who owns the follow-up.** No owner recorded.

**Worth holding onto.** Two organisations in Maningrida have bought from us, Homeland School Company and Mala'la Health Service. Repeat buyers in one place say more about demand than any count does. See [[communities/maningrida]].

## Door four · A community-controlled health service or council company

**The qualifying question.** Is this body accountable to the community it serves, and does it already carry the cost of the problem a bed answers?

**The proof invoice.** Mala'la Health Service Aboriginal Corporation, INV-0283. 13 Basket Beds, $5,434 including GST, delivered to Maningrida. The smallest invoice of the five, and the one that changed how freight is handled: $3,200 of shipping was quoted and discounted in full, so Goods carried freight worth 65% of the bed line. See [[trade/price-and-freight]].

**The next organisation that has asked.** Palm Island Community Company, 40 beds. The figure arrived in a partner update with no rule, no scope and no name behind it. Palm Island is also one of the working choices for a QBE plant, so this figure will be read as demand for that plant by anybody who sees the two together. It is a number from a partner update and carries its status wherever it goes. See [[communities/palm-island-picc]].

**Who owns the follow-up.** No owner recorded.

## Two acts that belong to people and to no door

A door is a kind of organisation. Two of the strongest things on the record were done by people, and they are printed as people.

- **Dianne Stokes, Warumungu Elder, Tennant Creek. 20 beds, money named.** She offered to fund the twenty herself. She also named the Pakkimjalki Kari washing machines in Warumungu, so this is a long relationship and not a first conversation. No invoice has been raised and no date has been set. This is the only record on the demand list with a person and a stated way of paying.
- **Norman Frank, Tennant Creek. 3 beds, a person asked.** Three beds in the maroon colourway. The smallest record on the list and the most specific. Somebody who has chosen a colour is further along than an enquiry about five hundred. See [[communities/tennant-creek]].

## The weakest rung, and why it is here

Simone Grimmond at WHSAC raised 500 beds in one meeting on the Groote Archipelago, alongside 300 washing machines. It is logged as exploring. Nothing has been quoted and no order has been discussed. It is the largest figure anyone holds and the weakest thing on the record, and it is printed with that status beside it or it is left out.

## What is missing

No door has a recorded owner. Five communities have asked, four kinds of buyer are proven, and nothing in either module names the person who makes the next call. That is the gap to close before this page can be used to run anything.

The second gap is a real count. One community doing four questions per household, done by a local person who is paid for it: how many sleep here, how many have a bed, how many are on the floor, who would use a new bed first. Until then every need figure we hold is an impression, including the good ones.
