# The QBE Stage 2 form, question by question

> Most of our surfaces work from a faithful paraphrase of the QBE Stage 2 form. This article works from the form itself, which sits in a toggle headed "Original supplied form, reference only" on the QBE final application page in Notion. Twenty five numbered questions: fifteen text fields, seven upload fields, one dollar field, and two declaration checkboxes. Nine of our answers are final: Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11, Q18 and Q23. Two of those nine answer a different question than the form asks, and both are known: Q14 and Q18 both turn on what kind of capital QBE would unlock. Everything else is waiting on a document somebody owes or a decision only a person can make. The form closes at noon on Friday 25 September 2026.

## The pool, and what the grant actually is

One pool of up to $1.1 million, shared across ten enterprises. Q5 caps a single request at $400,000. We ask $300,000, being 2 plants at the $150,000 allowance.

The form says what it is buying, in its own words at Q5:

> The funding pool will be allocated across 10 organisations. Please enter a single dollar amount (numbers only, no $ or commas), up to a maximum of $400,000. The catalytic effect of the grant - how much additional funding it unlocks - is a core part of the Steering Committee's assessment criteria.

**The grant is catalytic and discretionary. It is never a dollar-for-dollar match.** Ruling V, 1 August 2026, retired the match story. What the terms bind is the grant itself: it has to be at least matched by signed external commitments, which is a coverage test on whatever QBE decides to give. Raising money puts no obligation on QBE, and nothing guarantees a floor. The retired wording and where it still appears is held in [[capital/what-we-no-longer-say]].

Jay Boolkin's steer for 2026, recorded on the same Notion page, sharpens the Q5 criterion: QBE wants corporate philanthropy to unlock debt or equity, and does not want to use corporate philanthropy to unlock other corporate philanthropy. Our $600,000 asked is 100% philanthropy. That is the single largest gap between what the form rewards and what we are about to submit, and it is why the SEFA Backing the Bold EOI matters more than any wording change. See [[investors/sefa]].

## How the fields behave

Q1 and Q2 allow 20,000 characters each, so length constrains nothing anywhere in this form. Every upload field takes five files at 10MB each. If an upload fails, the form says to email the attachments to jay@socialimpacthub.org and adam@socialimpacthub.org.

Two fields ahead of Q1 carry the enterprise name and the primary contact's first name, last name, phone and email. They are not numbered in the supplied copy and are not counted in the 25.

One oddity in the supplied copy is worth knowing before you read the table. Q12 asks why governance documents are unavailable, and the field that uploads them is Q22, ten questions later. Read them as one pair, because one missing file blocks both.

## The nine that are final

Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11, Q18 and Q23. Eight of the nine are checker-clean and reconciled to the workbook. Q6 and Q7 were rewritten on the evening of 11 September against the siting ruling, so nothing is held waiting on a decision about where the plants go.

What those nine say, in short.

Q5 and Q7 are numbers: $300,000 for two plants, $150,000 for one. Q6 prices the plants module by module, $95,767 at the low end and $142,467 at the high, against a $150,000 allowance that sits above the high column so a harder pad or a barged container does not stop a build. It says a plant starts at 200 beds a year on a single press and reaches 720 on the same equipment as the local crew builds speed. It names the four siting tests: beds are already in the community, the community organisation has asked in its own words, somebody local wants to run it, and people there have cleared quotes publicly. Palm Island passes all four and goes first. Maningrida passes all four and goes second. Neither community has agreed to a build, and neither is told it is in a funding application before it has.

Q8 sets out every related-party connection, including Kristy Bloomfield's directorship of both Oonchiumpa and the applicant, and the Alice Springs plant that Oonchiumpa's Commonwealth money pays for outside this request.

Q10 and Q11 hold the impact answers. 320 beds bought and paid for by four organisations across five settled invoices, $273,966 including GST and $247,770 net. 540 beds on the register across eleven communities. One of the impact measures is counted and the rest are modelled, and the article says so. Health carries no number: scabies and the path it runs to rheumatic heart disease is why a bed is washable and off the ground, and the claim stops there.

Q23 lists the four capital-raising files and says plainly that nothing in our funding is signed.

## Where our answer answers a different question

Two rows above carry this flag. Both turn on the same thing, which is what kind of capital QBE would be unlocking.

### Q14, other funders

The form asks for four specific things where no commitment exists: the engagement, **the type of capital** the funder is considering, **which entity** would receive that capital, and **whether QBE funding is required to unlock or de-risk it**.

Our drafted answer lists who invited us and for how much, with dates and board meetings. It is accurate and it stops short of all four. It does not label each line by instrument, it does not say which entity receives each line, and it does not apply the unlock test line by line. It also carries ALIVE's bed purchase inside a funder answer. ALIVE at the University of Melbourne is a paid institutional buyer: $101,200 including GST and $92,000 net on INV-0342, for 100 Stretch Beds at $800 plus four half-share workshops at $3,000. That is trade income and it belongs in the trade record at [[trade/what-has-been-bought]], never in a list of catalytic capital. The $75,000 that appeared on an earlier deck was never on an invoice and is retired.

The rewrite is mechanical. Four columns for every line: funder, instrument, receiving entity, and what QBE unlocks or de-risks. Then one line on what the year costs and where it lands: the year needs $747,950, $600,000 is asked across five lines, $0 is secured, and the gap is $147,950.

Q14 also carries a disclosure duty nobody has discharged. A second Commonwealth $150,000 for a facility is judged highly likely. If it lands on Palm Island or Maningrida it funds an activity QBE is being asked to fund, and Q14 and Q15 have to carry it as other funding for the same purpose. If it lands on a third site, the application is untouched. Ben names the site, and that answer is worth $147,950.

### Q18, catalytic

The form asks what funding QBE would **unlock, accelerate or de-risk**, and why that funding would be unlikely to proceed in the same way without QBE.

Our final answer explains that the Brian M. Davis budget template pays out only once all project funding for a project is confirmed, so a QBE award helps confirm it. That is a real condition, quoted verbatim from the template's INSTRUCTIONS sheet, and it is a payment condition we would be satisfying. The form is asking what capital we would be unlocking. The answer also ends by putting a question back to QBE about what evidence can be accepted on 13 November while the other boards are still sitting. Asking it is right. Asking it inside the assessed answer field is a choice somebody should make on purpose.

The larger problem is the one Jay named. Everything Q18 currently unlocks is philanthropy: Tim Fairfax, Brian M. Davis and Snow are all grants. There is no debt and no equity in the $600,000.

The fix is already drafted. A11, the SEFA Backing the Bold EOI, is written and unsent, the Queensland stream is open with rolling acceptance, and a letter of intent does not have to be a signed deal. The loan can now be sized: the second press at $22,500 plus working capital at $276 a bed, which is the legacy making allowance and is provisional while the bought leg panel yield is unknown. At the provisional $474 a bed, 101 paid beds a year services a $200,000 facility, and that is inside one plant's output. Sending it is worth more to this application than any wording change on the form.

## What blocks submission, in order

1. **The accountant's letter.** The QBE final application page records it as the submission blocker and leaves the owner column empty. Everything else on this list has a name against it.
2. **Butterfly's constitution.** Unlocated. It blocks Q12 and Q22 together, and it also blocks Tim Fairfax 4.1, the 50% Indigenous business threshold and IBA eligibility. Eloise.
3. **The Backing the Bold EOI.** Not a blocker in form terms, and the thing most likely to change the outcome. Ben.
4. **Q19's six documents.** A site letter and a quote basis for each plant, and a cash milestone schedule. Nic.
5. **Current management cashflow and reconciled opening balances.** A programme forecast is not entity cashflow. Eloise.
6. **Kristy Bloomfield's related-party minute**, at the 14 September board, with a matching minute from Oonchiumpa.

Each of these is carried with what it is worth in [[capital/the-blockers]].

## One thing Q19 needs before it goes

The drafted Q19 text describes a line that presses both leg sheets and tab sheets at Witta and assembles beds here: three beds a day, 48 a month, 576 a year. Ben ruled the route differently on 12 September. Only tab sheets are pressed at Witta, leg sheets are bought, and flat-packed kits are dispatched for young people and others to assemble in community. Factory capacity ends at dispatch. The figures that replace the old ones are 6 kits a day, 16 run days a month at 80% availability, 96 kits a month and 1,152 a year.

The cost of a bed moved with the route and is now provisional. A bed sells at $750. The $276 making figure is a legacy allowance and the $474 contribution is provisional, because the yield of leg kits from a bought panel is unknown. Nic owns that yield, from the cutting evidence on the 25 sheets already invoiced. Break-even at 628 beds a year derives from the provisional $474, so it is provisional too and has to be written that way wherever it is printed.
