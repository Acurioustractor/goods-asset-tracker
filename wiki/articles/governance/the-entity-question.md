> half of it on 5 September 2026: The Butterfly Movement Ltd is the applicant and the recipient,
> Goods on Country is a registered business name of Butterfly, and the directors are the directors.
> The half still open is which entity holds the trade revenue. SEFA's Backing the Bold EOI carries a
> required field asking what percentage of revenue comes from trade, with EBITDA for FY26, FY25,

> about negative $42,854. The trading side carries roughly $168,000 before founder wages and every
> paid bed invoice Goods has ever raised. Ben, Nic and Joel own the decision and it is open.

## The numbers that force the decision

SEFA's EOI asks one question that cannot be answered without choosing an entity: what percentage of
revenue comes from trade, with four years of EBITDA behind it.

| | Butterfly | The trading side |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 income | $77,080 | about $713,827 on the Goods carve-out |
| FY26 result | expenses $119,934, so EBITDA about negative $42,854, closing cash $4,041 | roughly $168,000 before founder wages |
| Revenue from trade | almost none | all of the bed and washer invoices |
| FY23 to FY25 | a dormant shell | where the history is |

A lender reading four years of Butterfly sees one loss and three years of a shell. Applying as
Butterfly makes a loan look unfundable. Applying as the trading side makes it fundable and opens
the next question, which is which entity signs and which entity repays.

Two cautions travel with those figures. The $713,827 is a Goods-only carve-out prepared with the
accountant as a workpaper. No accountant has signed it, so the figure may be cited and the word
signed stays off it. The roughly $168,000 is
recorded in the 12 September review of the real forms and has no signed set of accounts under it
yet, which is the same gap the accountant's letter is meant to close.

Joel flagged this exact problem on 21 August and offered a call with Tanya from SEFA's investment
team to look at the financials. That call belongs before the EOI goes.

## The trade record is real and it is in the other entity

The reason the question bites is that Goods has a trade record worth showing. Four organisations
have bought and paid for 320 beds across five settled invoices: $273,966 including GST, $247,770
net. Facilitation is a second thing buyers already pay for, $50,000 net across ten visits billed at
$3,000, $6,000 and $8,000. ALIVE at the University of Melbourne paid $101,200 including GST for 100
Stretch Beds and four half-share workshops on INV-0342.

None of that paper is Butterfly's. It was invoiced out of the sole trader's ledger and is moving
across under the transfer described at QBE Q8. So the applicant of record holds the least trade and
the entity with the trade is the one ruling AA did not choose.

## The constitution is the unread document

Butterfly's constitution has not been located. It is recorded against Eloise. Four separate things
wait on it:

1. QBE question 12, which is blocked on the file itself, and question 22, which is owed a document.
2. Tim Fairfax field 4.1 on the FY27 Resilience form, which is required. TFFF also lists the
   constitution among fourteen required attachments, of which Goods holds three.
3. The 50% test that decides whether Goods is an Indigenous business. `ownership-problem.ts` states
   it plainly: the same 50% threshold gates Indigenous Business Australia finance, where it reads as
   ownership or membership, and for a company limited by guarantee the test is membership, which
   sits in Butterfly's constitution and has not been read.
4. IBA eligibility, which turns on that same membership clause.

Ruling J drew the line that must never blur. Aboriginal directors on the charity is not First
Nations ownership of the entity that sells. Supply Nation, the Indigenous Procurement Policy, IBA
and First Australians Capital all test the supplier, which is the company. Claiming the board
satisfies the supplier test in a funder document would end the relationship. Ownership is written
as a pathway on every surface.

## Why this one question reaches all three applications

QBE's Q2 asks five things in one field: which entity applies, how the related entities connect, how
funds flow, why the grant sits where it sits, and how capital raised elsewhere supports the
enterprise. Every one of those five is an entity answer.

Tim Fairfax invited Butterfly by name. Katie Norman wrote on 31 August, "invite The Butterfly
Movement to apply for a three-year grant of $300,000.00 in three equal payments", and the FY27
Resilience form tests governance more than it tests the model.

SEFA forces the question outright, and SEFA is the one that matters most this month. Jay's steer on
the QBE final application page is that corporate philanthropy is meant to unlock debt or equity this
year, and the $600,000 Goods has asked for is 100% philanthropy. The only debt line available is the
SEFA loan. A sensible first ask there is $50,000 to $100,000: the second press at $22,500 plus
working capital. At the provisional contribution of $474 a bed, 101 paid beds a year services
$200,000, and provisional is the right word until the bought leg panel yield is counted.

So the entity answer sits on the critical path of a philanthropic application, a governance
application and a loan at once. It is a decision for Ben, Nic and Joel, and this article does not
make it.
