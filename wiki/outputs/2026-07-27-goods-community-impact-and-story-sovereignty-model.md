# Goods Community Impact and Story Sovereignty Model

> **Status:** proposed operating model, grounded in the Goods and Empathy Ledger repositories as at 27 July 2026. This document joins the current Goods strategy, cost model, community pathway, asset register, voice-led impact work and Empathy Ledger governance into one verifiable method. It does not replace `STRATEGY.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, `canon.ts` or `asset-canonical.ts`.

## The proposition

Goods is not only delivering beds and equipment. It is testing whether a community can:

1. define what a useful good and a good outcome mean locally;
2. receive, use, repair and account for essential goods;
3. collect material and make products On Country;
4. build the skills, demand, governance and operating systems around the making;
5. move closer to owning the assets, decisions, revenue and evidence;
6. tell its own story about what changed, what did not and what should happen next; and
7. leave a governed case study that another community can learn from without being told to copy it.

Empathy Ledger is the story and evidence infrastructure for this work. It should not become the owner of community knowledge or the authority that decides what impact means. Its job is to preserve provenance, consent, cultural governance, verification, approved use, withdrawal and return.

The model in one sentence:

> **A Goods case study is a community-controlled record of intent, delivery, use, capability, ownership and learning, backed by asset and operating evidence, carried by consented voices, and returned to the community before any external use.**

## What is verified, inferred and still unknown

### Verified in the repositories

- Goods has a six-stage public pathway: **Yarn, Shape, Resource, Deliver, Transfer, Grow**.
- The cost model now prices a site base plus selectable modules rather than assuming every community needs a complete plant.
- Utopia and Oonchiumpa can be priced using the current module model. Tennant Creek and Palm Island remain blocked on community and governance decisions that should not be guessed.
- Community ownership is a pathway. It is not a completed state.
- Goods distinguishes a network cost centre, a site production cost centre and a site wraparound cost centre.
- The current economic case is modelled, not demonstrated at sustained production. The measured production run remains essential.
- Goods has an asset register, canonical public figures, confidence labels and drift checks.
- Goods has a twin-spine impact approach: numbers show scale; voices show meaning.
- The current voice analysis found strong evidence around supply-chain failure, family, Country, dignity, community-led design and reciprocity. Making, jobs and the ownership pathway have a thinner voice base and need deliberate field evidence.
- Empathy Ledger has implemented consent routing, withdrawal exclusion, verbatim excerpt grounding, cultural sensitivity gates, Elder review requirements and trust states for claims.
- Empathy Ledger supports interviews, transcripts, storyteller records, outcome records, case-study review, media governance, syndication and storyteller-facing story-use records.
- Processing permission is not publication permission.
- Sensitive or sacred material and Elder-pending material must not be automatically published.
- Goods' scabies to rheumatic heart disease pathway is the reason for the work, not a measured Goods health outcome.

### Inferred from the combined work

- The Goods production pathway and the Empathy Ledger story pathway are the same governance problem expressed through different assets.
- A plant is not community-owned merely because it sits in community. A story is not community-owned merely because it is about community.
- Transfer has to be evidenced across several kinds of control: physical assets, operating decisions, money, knowledge, data and public narrative.
- The best unit for tracking impact is not only a bed, interview or project. It is a **community decision cycle**: intent, action, evidence, review, return and next decision.
- The most credible replication product is not a fixed facility blueprint. It is a governed case-study packet that lets another community see the choices, costs, conditions, evidence and unresolved questions.
- Empathy Ledger can be the verification layer for qualitative evidence if it remains subordinate to storyteller and community authority.

### Unknown or unresolved

- What exact ownership structure each community wants.
- Which assets each community wants first and which it does not want.
- Who pays and supervises the production line in each site.
- What level of demand sustains each community configuration.
- Measured labour time, yield, cost, maintenance and quality at a sustained production rate.
- The locally chosen indicators for health, dignity, youth participation, cultural continuity and economic value.
- Which partner is authorised to verify any clinical or population health outcome.
- How community analysis and publication approval will be resourced, paid and recorded.
- What technical hosting and data-residency option each community will choose over time.

These unknowns are not defects to hide. They define the next conversations and measurements.

## The philosophy

### 1. The community leads the definition of success

Goods may bring prompts, comparable measures and operating knowledge. It does not pre-fill the answer. Each community states:

- what problem it wants to address;
- what it wants to make possible;
- what a useful first step looks like;
- what should be measured;
- who has authority to interpret the evidence;
- what may be shared; and
- what decision the evidence should inform.

### 2. Delivery is the beginning of measurement

A delivery count is a verified output. It is not automatically an outcome.

The impact question starts after arrival:

- Is the product present and usable?
- Is it being used as intended?
- Does it survive local conditions?
- Can it be cleaned, repaired and maintained?
- Who has to make the system work?
- What changed for households, workers and organisations?
- What unexpected effects appeared?
- What should happen next?

### 3. No number without a voice, no voice reduced to a number

Numbers and testimony do different jobs.

- The asset register proves what was delivered, where, when and in what state.
- Production records prove materials, time, cost, quality, training and maintenance.
- Voices explain meaning, context, experience, authority and change.
- Outcome records state the claim, indicator, method, evidence, verifier and confidence.

A quote must not be converted into a prevalence claim. A count must not be presented as lived meaning.

### 4. Evidence travels with provenance and permission

Every external claim should resolve back to:

- the community and place;
- the person or system record that produced it;
- the source record and version;
- the exact excerpt, event or measurement;
- the consent and cultural review state;
- the audience and purpose approved;
- the reviewer or verifier;
- the date and confidence label; and
- the current withdrawal or expiry state.

### 5. Return before publication

The first audience for a community's evidence is the community.

Before a funder report, website story, deck, newsletter or replication case study:

1. return the source and draft synthesis in an accessible form;
2. let storytellers correct, remove or contextualise their contribution;
3. let the community review the interpretation and proposed claims;
4. record the approved audience and purpose;
5. preserve minority or dissenting views where appropriate; and
6. publish only the approved version.

### 6. Transfer is multidimensional

Ownership should be tracked as a progression, not a badge.

| Dimension of control | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Assets | Who owns the equipment today? What is the documented transfer path? |
| Operations | Who decides production, maintenance, staffing and pause points? |
| Money | Who invoices, holds revenue, pays wages and decides reinvestment? |
| Capability | Who can safely operate, repair, train and assure quality without Goods present? |
| Demand | Who holds customer relationships, contracts and offtake decisions? |
| Knowledge and IP | What can the community adapt, teach and take to another provider? |
| Data | Where are records held, who can access them and who can withdraw them? |
| Narrative | Who analyses, approves and publishes the story of the work? |

The pathway is only as strong as its weakest control. Equipment transfer without decision power is not full ownership. Story custody without interpretive authority is not story sovereignty.

## The Goods evidence stack

### Layer 1: Community authority

Record:

- lead community-controlled organisation;
- cultural and operational authorities;
- participating groups and affected people;
- decision protocol;
- safeguarding requirements;
- recording and sharing rules;
- dispute, pause and withdrawal process; and
- who approves the final community record.

### Layer 2: Baseline and intent

Create a short community-owned baseline before delivery or a new production module:

- aspiration in the community's words;
- existing assets, skills, programs and organisations;
- current access, freight, failure and repair conditions;
- current production or employment activity;
- existing material and waste flows;
- demand and potential customers;
- locally meaningful outcome questions; and
- what is already working and must not be displaced.

### Layer 3: Delivery and asset truth

The Goods asset register remains the source for:

- asset identifier and product;
- destination community and custodian;
- date, funding source and delivery event;
- status and condition;
- repairs, retirement and loss;
- linked photos, video and documents; and
- the claim status of any derived public figure.

This layer answers **what arrived and what exists**, not whether community wellbeing improved.

### Layer 4: Production and capability truth

For each run or module, capture:

- selected module configuration;
- equipment ownership and operator;
- local and visiting labour hours;
- paid roles, unpaid participation and training hours, kept separate;
- material input, yield, offcut and waste;
- cycle and setup time;
- unit cost and full site cost;
- energy, freight, maintenance and downtime;
- quality results and failures;
- products made, repaired and sold;
- revenue, wages and reinvestment; and
- Goods support required.

Every value carries one of the Goods honesty labels: verified, workpaper, modelled, target, conflict or retired.

### Layer 5: Story and testimony truth

Empathy Ledger holds the governed qualitative evidence:

- storyteller identity or approved attribution;
- community, project and role;
- interview or field-recording source;
- transcript version and source fingerprint;
- AI-processing permission;
- cultural sensitivity and Elder review;
- verbatim excerpt with source location;
- theme or outcome relationship;
- exact-use approval by audience and purpose;
- media people-tags and asset links;
- withdrawal state; and
- where the story has been used.

### Layer 6: Outcome claim

An outcome record should not be publishable until it contains:

- outcome statement;
- domain;
- who defined it;
- indicator and unit;
- baseline or comparison, if relevant;
- observation period;
- method;
- evidence references;
- contribution statement;
- counter-evidence or limitations;
- verification status;
- named verifier;
- confidence label;
- community approval; and
- next review date.

### Layer 7: Case study and return

The approved case study is a view over the previous layers, not a manually rewritten evidence island.

It contains:

- community context and authority;
- aspiration and starting point;
- configuration chosen;
- what was delivered and done;
- verified outputs;
- experienced changes in approved voices;
- measured or verified outcomes;
- economics and capability;
- ownership movement;
- what did not work;
- unknowns and limitations;
- the community's next decision;
- approved reusable lessons; and
- provenance and use conditions.

## The impact domains

The current five Goods domains remain useful as a common comparison frame:

1. **Rest and health**
2. **Dignity and safety**
3. **Indigenous self-determination and community-led design**
4. **Jobs, On Country work and the path to ownership**
5. **Circular and local economy**

They should be treated as a shared index, not a compulsory local theory. A community can add, rename, reject or prioritise domains in its own record.

Two through-lines apply to all five:

- **Economics:** where cost, value, work, revenue and risk move.
- **Sovereignty:** where authority, knowledge, data and decisions sit.

## A practical measurement set

### A. Delivery and use

| Measure | Evidence | Claim level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assets delivered and active | Asset register and status history | Verified |
| Assets still functional at review | Condition check | Verified |
| Repair, loss and retirement rate | Service and status events | Verified |
| Use pattern and fit | Approved observation and testimony | Testimony or measured sample |
| Repeat request or order | CRM, order or approved community record | Verified demand signal |

### B. Making and capability

| Measure | Evidence | Claim level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products made locally | Production batch record | Verified |
| Material used and diverted | Weighed batch input and output | Verified when weighed, modelled otherwise |
| Paid local labour hours and wages | Timesheet and payroll | Verified |
| Training and independent competency | Training and assessment record | Verified |
| Unit time, yield, cost and quality | Measured run | Verified for that run |
| Goods support hours per run | Support log | Verified |
| Local maintenance completed | Maintenance record | Verified |

### C. Ownership and enterprise

| Measure | Evidence | Claim level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assets under community control | Agreement and asset title | Verified |
| Decisions held locally | Governance record and decision log | Verified for named decisions |
| Revenue invoiced by community entity | Invoice and ledger | Verified |
| Wages and value retained locally | Payroll and accounts | Verified |
| Customer and offtake relationships held locally | Contract or CRM record | Verified |
| Functions completed without Goods | Operating record | Verified |
| Agreed next transfer milestone | Community decision | Target |

### D. Lived outcomes and story

| Measure | Evidence | Claim level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Locally defined change | Approved interview or group review | Testimony |
| Theme present across independent voices | Governed coding with source traceability | Evidence pattern, not prevalence |
| Most significant change | Community-selected story and rationale | Participatory finding |
| Difference between expected and experienced change | Baseline and follow-up review | Contribution finding |
| Story returned and reviewed | Return event and decisions | Verified |
| Story uses visible to storyteller | Use-and-return ledger | Verified |
| Withdrawals honoured | Audit log and downstream action | Verified |

### E. Health and wellbeing

Health should remain a partner-led stream.

Goods can verify beds, washers, condition, use, maintenance and community testimony. A clinical or population outcome requires:

- a community-authorised health partner;
- an agreed method and baseline;
- an appropriate observation period;
- ethics, privacy and cultural governance;
- a contribution rather than automatic attribution model; and
- publication approval from the relevant authority.

Until then, the scabies to rheumatic heart disease relationship remains the rationale, not the result.

## The interview system

### Interview groups

Do not ask one person to stand in for a whole community. Build a small, governed evidence circle:

- Elders and cultural authorities;
- community organisation and governance leaders;
- makers, operators and maintainers;
- young people and participants, with safeguarding;
- households or product users;
- delivery and service partners;
- Goods staff or technical partners; and
- funders or external witnesses, clearly labelled as such.

### Interview moments

1. **Before:** what is happening now, what matters, what should change and who decides?
2. **At delivery or first making:** what was chosen, what happened and what does it mean today?
3. **Early use:** what fits, fails, surprises or needs changing?
4. **Measured production run:** what did the work actually require?
5. **Ownership review:** what decisions and functions have moved, and what has not?
6. **Community return:** is this interpretation fair, useful and safe to share?
7. **Later follow-up:** what endured, changed direction or stopped?

### Founder and leadership interviews

Founder and leadership material is useful for:

- intent and historical decisions;
- cost and operating assumptions;
- relationship history;
- failures and corrections;
- what Goods believes its role should become; and
- what would make Goods less central over time.

It must not be used to speak for community experience or community authority. Leadership claims should be tested against operational records and community voices.

### Core prompts

- What did you want to make possible?
- Who asked for this and who had authority to decide?
- What was happening before?
- What did you choose to try, and why this first?
- What changed in practice?
- What did not change?
- What was harder or more costly than expected?
- Who did the work and who was paid?
- Who now makes which decisions?
- What can happen without Goods being present?
- What do you want to own or control next?
- What should other communities understand, without assuming their answer will be the same?
- What from this conversation may be analysed, returned, quoted or published, for which audience?

## The community case-study packet

Each community packet should be generated from structured records and approved narrative.

### Public layer

- one-page community-approved story;
- approved voices and media;
- verified asset and production facts;
- clear confidence labels;
- ownership pathway state;
- lessons the community has approved for sharing; and
- contact or next-step pathway chosen by the community.

### Community layer

- source interviews and recordings;
- transcripts and corrections;
- full asset and operating history;
- evidence map;
- review notes and dissent;
- approvals, restrictions and withdrawal controls;
- story-use ledger; and
- next-decision record.

### Funder or partner layer

- theory of change for this configuration;
- outputs, outcomes and limitations;
- complete-project costs;
- production and wraparound pots;
- risk and governance;
- contribution analysis;
- evidence references; and
- reporting period.

### Replication layer

- what the community started with;
- modules selected and rejected;
- prerequisites and authority;
- cost ranges and cost drivers;
- staffing and support;
- failure points;
- measured run results;
- ownership milestones;
- questions another community must answer for itself; and
- reusable tools, not prescribed conclusions.

## System roles

### Goods Asset Register

Source of truth for physical assets, delivery, condition, repairs, production batches and public canonical counts.

### Goods cost and pathway model

Source of truth for module configurations, modelled economics, complete-project costs, selected pathway and transfer milestones.

### Empathy Ledger

Source of truth for governed interviews, transcripts, storytellers, verbatim evidence, cultural review, exact-use permission, outcome evidence references, case-study approval and story use-and-return.

### Community-controlled organisation

Authority for local purpose, interpretation, decision-making, approval, access, publication and the next step.

### Independent or domain partner

Verifier for claims outside Goods' authority, including clinical health, formal evaluation, accounts, safety or technical quality.

## What previous Empathy Ledger work teaches us

### Keep

- Default-deny consent routing.
- Withdrawal exclusion.
- Verbatim grounding and source locations.
- Sensitive, sacred and Elder review gates.
- Trust states that separate approved, pending, culturally withheld and unattributed material.
- Transcript versioning and provenance.
- Storyteller self-access and use-and-return.
- Case-study reviewer decisions and release gates.
- Separate private governance analysis from public release.

### Correct

- Do not treat project membership or a matching name as proof of provenance.
- Do not count duplicate transcripts held by multiple organisations as additional evidence.
- Do not treat AI-processing consent, a story consent flag or a blanket verbal assurance as exact publication approval.
- Do not treat an outcome row labelled `verified` as verified without evidence, verifier and date.
- Do not let model-generated themes or quotes become findings without source review.
- Do not let a Goods-side display cache become the canonical consent source.
- Do not publish the five Goods domains as though they were chosen by every community.

### Stop

- Output-only impact dashboards.
- Health or justice attribution without an authorised method.
- Quotes with no resolvable person and source.
- Combined storyteller records when separate consent and attribution are required.
- Founder narrative presented as community evidence.
- Case studies written after the fact from scattered documents.
- Replication language that implies every community should build the same facility.

## The first pilot

Use one community pathway as the end-to-end proof. Tennant Creek is the strongest operational candidate because the existing work already includes a Shed proposal, Youth Centre relationship, deep design history, Goods assets and a substantial voice corpus. The pilot still requires community agreement.

### Pilot sequence

1. Confirm the lead organisation, authorities and decision protocol.
2. Reconcile existing asset, proposal, interview and media records.
3. Ask the community to define the purpose and locally meaningful outcomes.
4. Select the smallest useful module and complete-project budget.
5. Create baseline, ownership and evidence-return records.
6. Deliver or run the module.
7. Measure cost, time, material, quality, participation, pay and support.
8. Conduct early-use and maker interviews.
9. Review evidence in community.
10. Record the next ownership decision.
11. Publish only the approved case-study layer.
12. Package the reusable questions, tools and measured conditions for another community.

### Pilot success test

The pilot succeeds when the community can answer:

- what happened;
- what changed and what did not;
- what it cost;
- who did the work and who benefited;
- which claims are verified and which remain testimony or model;
- what the community now controls;
- what it wants to do next;
- what it permits others to learn; and
- where every published statement came from.

## Build priorities

### Priority 0: Governance and claim integrity

- Reconcile Goods storytellers, transcripts, duplicate holdings and project links.
- Replace blanket consent assumptions with named-use grants.
- Complete source review for any quote or theme proposed for external use.
- Ensure every public voice resolves to Empathy Ledger provenance and current permission.
- Keep cultural review stricter than ordinary consent.

### Priority 1: Join the records

- Add stable IDs linking community, asset, production run, transcript, outcome, claim and case study.
- Add ownership-control milestones to the community pathway.
- Add evidence-return and community-approval events.
- Make case studies render from governed structured records.

### Priority 2: Measure the making

- Run the sustained production study.
- Measure time, yield, cost, energy, quality, maintenance and support.
- Separate paid work, training, volunteering and program participation.
- Add makers and operators to the interview corpus.

### Priority 3: Community analysis

- Fund community review and interpretation time.
- Build a low-bandwidth return and correction experience.
- Allow communities to set local domains and approve mappings to the shared Goods frame.
- Make story use visible and withdrawal actionable.

### Priority 4: Replication library

- Produce the first approved end-to-end case study.
- Create a comparison view across communities that preserves differences.
- Publish tools, questions, cost drivers and failure lessons.
- Never publish a universal ownership threshold or a one-size facility blueprint.

## The north-star test

For every product, system, metric, interview, report and funding decision, ask:

> **Does this move the community closer to controlling the making, the decisions, the value and the story, or does it make Goods and Empathy Ledger more central?**

If the system produces better evidence but community cannot see it, correct it, govern it, use it or take it elsewhere, it has not completed the work.
