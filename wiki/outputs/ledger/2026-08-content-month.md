### Week 1 (Aug 3) - where it began

**Mon (quote card, Dianne portrait):**
"It means something that really makes me happy. Every time I go away, it's like it's calling me. Come back home."
Dianne Stokes, Warumungu and Warlmanpa Elder, on Pakkimjalki Kari, the washing machine she named.

**Wed (model note, text-led):**
The products did not arrive in community. They started there. The Stretch Bed and Pakkimjalki Kari were designed and named in Tennant Creek, with Elders like Dianne Stokes. That is the first thing to know about the model: the design authority sits in community, and everything else follows from it. This month we are walking you through how, one person and one place at a time.

**Fri (photo, alfred-johnson.jpg or bedding-golden-hour.jpg, Palm Island):**
"You have to bring them on the barge. You can't just take them on the boat. You have to pay for freight. It all adds up." Alfred Johnson, Palm Island. The freight arithmetic is why making things closer to home is the whole idea.

### Week 2 (Aug 10) - the making

**Mon (quote card, Mykel portrait):**
"Never would have thought it would have come out like that."
Mykel, on the Stretch Bed he built in Alice Springs. He kept it. Every young person who built one did.

**Wed (model note):**
Two days behind the Oonchiumpa office, young people built beds from flat-pack components. The first thing the activity produced was theirs to keep. The second thing was a question: what if the making itself stayed local? Mykel's answer: "Yeah, I'll be rocking up every day to make them." Gary, who leads a men's group in Mount Isa, put the same idea his own way: "We could even teach them down there to make it... and they can take the skill to make it. And they, well, take pride, that's my bed, you know."

**Fri (video post, Mykel build video /video/partners/oonchiumpa/mykel-building-the-bed.mp4, cleared):**
Watch Mykel build his bed. 90 seconds, no voiceover needed.

### Week 3 (Aug 17) - the choosing

**Mon (quote card, scene photo utopia-09 with honest caption):**
"Good for me and comfy, easy to put together."
Dorrie Jones, Arlparra. Pictured: a Stretch Bed being tested in a Utopia Homelands home.

**Wed (model note):**
There is no standard package. Urapuntja is exploring one shredder. Tennant Creek, one small activity through the existing Shed. Oonchiumpa has asked for the whole production pathway. Palm Island asked us to start by listening. Same model, four different answers, because the community writes the order form.

**Fri (photo, utopia-01.jpg, beds arriving):**
May 2026, the road east into the homelands. Local teams named the households. Beds went family by family. 147 now in community at Utopia.
(147: per-community ledger, canon.)

### Week 4 (Aug 24) - the ownership

**Mon (quote card, Jahvan portrait):**
"There's gotta be a break in the cycle somewhere. Soon as you break the cycle, all this stuff stop."
Jahvan Oui, Palm Island. Goods team member. Training to run production himself.

**Wed (model note, the honesty device):**
Here is the test we hold ourselves to at every future production site, six months in: does the community hold the keys, is a local person on payroll, does a community entity invoice the buyer, does the community decide what gets made. Four of four or it is a fail. No site has passed yet, because no site is in production for sale yet. We publish the test so you can hold us to it.

**Fri (photo, whole-run-at-sunset.jpg, Maningrida):**
Forty beds in one community, our biggest build, pressed in-house and built with the Gamardi community in July. Proof the process works. The next question is who owns it, and that is the whole point.
(40: register, canon beds-deployed definition.)
