The model in five layers

Goods on Country transfers manufacturing and economic ownership into communities, using beds as the first proven product. The output of the work is community-owned enterprises. Success is measured by enterprises operating and by revenue kept locally. Bed counts are the input.

One bed

A bed sells for $750 at the factory door. $276 makes it, with the person on the press inside that number. $474 stays with Goods and pays for rent, the founders and everything else. Freight is charged on top, at cost, depending on where the bed goes.

Three pots

Every dollar Goods spends sits in one of three pots, and each pot has one kind of money that pays for it. Keeping them apart is what stopped today's "half a million to raise" from being real.

1 · Production

Making beds ($276 each), freight on top, and the production network Matt priced at $109,500 a year: production lead, admin, field travel, Witta rent.

Paid by

The bed price. $474 a bed. 231 beds a year cover the network.

Test: does it pay for itself at volume? Yes, above 231 beds.

2 · Community wraparound

At a community plant: the coordinator or manager, trainer and WHS, the youth programme, governance, story and evidence.

Paid by

Grants, by design. DEWR at Alice Springs. Brian M. Davis for facilitation. Future community grants.

Test: it never pays back and should not. The jobs are the dividend.

3 · The organisation

Founders beyond production days, accounting, marketing, the rest of the $297,550 a year it costs to be Goods.

Paid by

Everything Goods sells and is supported with, plus Tim Fairfax's operating support. Never raised.

Test: is the whole business solvent? Last year: $653,246 Goods-tagged in; whole ledger $1.46M in, $260K kept.

Who pays for this year's 400 beds

Two different layers, and they are never added together. Catalytic capital pays Goods $750 a bed for the first trading stock, so a community enterprise has something to sell. Institutional buyers then pay the community enterprise for those beds, and that money stays with the community. It is not Goods revenue.

What a community plant costs

A community does not buy "a facility". It chooses modules in feed order, on a site base. Matt priced each module; three of them are evidenced from the gear Goods already runs at Witta.

Capacity, and when to add a plant

NM's sheet, stated plainly: the hot press makes six sheets a day. A bed needs two, one tab sheet and one leg sheet, so that is three beds a day. Five days a week, forty-eight weeks a year with four weeks off: 720 beds a year at Witta. If Defy supplies the leg panels, Witta presses only the tab sheet and makes 30 a week, 1,440 a year, at a higher cost per bed.
