2 · What is still to raise

Only $75,000 of this year is actually money

The year needs $856,046. Of that, $75,000 is in the bank, being ALIVE's payment. Half a million is invited but unsigned, and all three of those boards sit in November. A further $150,000 has not been asked for yet. $97,500 is a quote a community is still thinking about. And $33,546 has no source at all. Read left to right, the bar goes from certain to imaginary.

The one decision that moves the most. Snow gave $375,000 of last year's Goods income and has no ask in front of them. The plan already spends their $100,000. Sal has booked a catch-up. Making that ask is the highest-value hour in the next fortnight and it is not a QBE task.

4 · Where the work is

Thirty-one communities on the record

Nine are active relationships, one is testing, one is exploring a large order, fifteen are named prospects with a partner organisation behind each, and five are administrative entries, so they do not count as communities. Five have told us they want a production facility. This is the map the deck has never had.

5 · Demand and buyers

885 beds asked for, 320 paid so far

Two different things, and the deck must never add them. On the left is demand: what communities and organisations have asked for, dated and attributed, none of it an order. On the right is what has actually been paid for and delivered. The gap between them is the case for the raise, and the reason the beds are made before the buyer pays.

The buyer story the deck has not told. Groote Archipelago asked about 500 beds and 300 washing machines through the WHSAC procurement pathway. That single conversation is larger than this whole year's plan. It is exploring, not an order, and it belongs in the demand evidence for Q10. Keep it out of every revenue line.
6 · What the storytellers say

390 stories, and they are not about recycling

The Empathy Ledger holds 390 stories in the Goods project: 14 published, 24 substantive drafts, the rest archived field captures. When the themes are counted, the community does not talk about the four areas the way the deck does. They talk about resilience, housing sovereignty and connection to Country. Recycling barely registers as a value; it is our materials fact. It is not the reason they give.

7 · Three, five, ten years

Goods that heal, made where the money stays

The goods that arrive in remote communities today are made somewhere else, freighted at enormous cost, break, and go to the tip. The money leaves with them. The mission is to replace them with goods designed on Country, made on Country, where the money stays. Impact investing has one job in that: building the capacity of a community to get there, and then getting out of the way.
