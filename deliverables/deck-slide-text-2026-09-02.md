## 01 Who can make the next one (`cgjeR`)

- The next bed should be made on Country.
- Our goal is simple: communities collect local plastic, make useful goods and own the work.

## 02 The imported supply system (`cJl95`)

- THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM. Remote communities import the goods and export the value.
- The current system brings products in. It creates little local work or ownership, and used plastic still goes to landfill.
- HEALTH. Crowding puts pressure on homes. 51.3%. In 2021, 51.3% of First Nations households in very remote NT needed at least one extra bedroom. In 2024, 1 in 93 people in the NT lived with ARF/RHD, the highest prevalence recorded in Australia.
- COMMUNITY ENTERPRISE. Few people run their own business. 3.1%. Only 3.1% of employed First Nations people in the NT managed their own business in 2021. This was the lowest share of any jurisdiction.
- EMPLOYMENT. Opportunity falls sharply with remoteness. 38.1%. First Nations employment was 38.1% in very remote Australia and 68.0% in major cities in 2022 to 23.
- WASTE. Recoverable material still goes to landfill. 275,190t. The NT sent 275,190 tonnes of waste to landfill in 2023 to 24, or 56.4% of all waste processed. Only 12.7% was recycled.
- The current system delivers products. It does little to build local ownership.
- Sources: ABS 2021 Census; AIHW ARF/RHD 2024 and employment 2022 to 23; NT Government waste report 2023 to 24. Enterprise figure is a proxy for the local ownership gap.

## 07 Governance before capital (`LhlJr`)

- THE GOVERNANCE BRIDGE. Goods on Country owns and runs the whole model.
- The assets and responsibility are transferred into Goods on Country. Each community keeps the local decisions.
- GOODS ON COUNTRY. One Indigenous-led organisation. PRODUCTS, IP, MAKING, SALES, DELIVERY, CAPITAL, GOVERNANCE. The products, assets, contracts, money and accountability are transferred into Goods on Country and run through it.
- COMMUNITY PARTNER. Decides what happens locally. BEDS, WORK, INCOME, EQUIPMENT, WHAT COMES NEXT. Each community decides how beds are used, who is paid and what is made next.
- One organisation holds the work. Local decisions stay local.

## 08 First capital becomes local choice (`BzO2h`)

- 02 AGREE THE RULES. Set the rules before beds move. Agree access, sales, paid work and where any sales money goes.
- 03 COMMUNITY RUNS THE POOL. Five local pools of 200 beds. Each community can change the mix when local needs change.

## 09 One governed pool, three uses (`slq40`)

- ONE COMMUNITY, ONE GOVERNED POOL. Each community decides how its bed pool works.
- Some beds can meet immediate need. Others can be sold to pay for local work or the next batch.
- START WITH 200 BEDS. Up to $150K gross sales value at $750 each. **(to delete)**
- LOCAL RULES SET THE MIX. Who receives a bed? How many are sold? Who is paid? Where does the money go? THE RULE: the mix can change when local needs change.
- ALLOCATE SOME, meet immediate need. SELL SOME, pay local work. REINVEST SOME, fund the next step.
- THEN THE COMMUNITY DECIDES AGAIN. Only sold beds create sales income. Before work starts, agree who keeps that money, what repays the first funding and what is reinvested.

## 08B Optional three kinds of money (`Lnlxh`)

- WORKING CONCEPT, THE FUNDING STACK. Three kinds of money. Three different jobs.
- 01 GIFTS. Lower the price of beds and pay for training, supervision and early testing. MORE PEOPLE CAN ACCESS A BED.
- 02 PURCHASES. Buy beds for community, create real demand and generate income from delivery and sales. BEDS MOVE + LOCAL INCOME GROWS.
- 03 LONG-TERM INVESTMENT. Pay for equipment, materials and stock. Repayment comes from future production and sales. PRODUCTION CAN GROW.
- Working concept: the amount, timing and repayment terms still need to be agreed with each partner.

### 08C The loop, in one drawing (`JCreO`)

- THE MODEL, ONE COMMUNITY, ONE LOOP, SHOWN ×5. One catalyst starts five loops a community controls.
- The funder acts once. The Goods on Country board holds the rules. Each community decides what is given, what is sold, who is paid and what the sales money builds next.
- THE CATALYST, ONCE. A funder backs 1,000 beds ($750,000 is the cost of the beds at $750 each; not sales, not community income). The Goods on Country board holds the rules (purpose, shared assets, appointments, reinvestment; Indigenous-led today; ownership stays a pathway). Five community pools of 200 beds, each held by a community partner under rules agreed before any bed moves. ×5 into the loop.
- ONE COMMUNITY LOOP. 1 200 beds arrive (cost $150,000). 2 The community sets the mix (give some, sell some). 3 Sales money stays local (up to $150,000 if all 200 sell at $750; less when beds are given; a design number until the rules are agreed). 4 The community decides what comes next (more beds, a shredder or a press, the washer; a full facility is $150,000 to $220,000, so one pool sold in full reaches the bottom of that band and no further). 5 Making moves closer (about $324 stays on a bed pressed locally against about $65 on a kit; modelled, not yet measured). Then the community decides again; the return arrow stays inside the community, never back to the funder.
- FOUR GATES BEFORE THE LOOP IS REAL. Buyers (who is buying the sold beds, named). Rules (allocation, sales money, resale and stock, agreed and signed). An operator and a place (who runs the line, where, and who pays them). A measured cost (fifty beds pressed at production rate, timed and costed).
- Working proposal. The rules for sales money, resale and who holds the beds are being agreed with each community. Until they are, the numbers are a design, not a promise.

### 09C What changes, and how we know (`qD5SQ`)

- IMPACT, WHAT CHANGES, AND HOW WE KNOW. Four things change. Each one is counted, and each one has a voice.
- Numbers prove scale and voices prove meaning. No number without a voice, no voice reduced to a number. Scabies and rheumatic heart disease are the reason the hardware matters; they are never claimed as an outcome.
- WHAT CHANGES. Access to health hardware (beds off the ground, washable and repairable, in homes where 51.3% of very remote NT First Nations households need another bedroom; counted per bed and per washer). Paid local work (assembly, delivery, repair and recycling; counted in hours and roles, not promised in headcounts). Local enterprise and control (a community partner holds a pool, sets the rules, keeps the sales money; tested at month six: keys, payroll, who invoices, half of production, partial counts as no). Plastic kept in use (about 20kg of recycled HDPE in every frame; a design figure today, weighed batch by batch in the measured run).
- HOW WE KNOW. The register (540 beds across 11 communities, a product passport per unit as the pools move). The voices (Empathy Ledger interviews, theme-coded and consented quote by quote; thirty-seven people cleared to speak externally). The measured run (fifty beds at production rate: hours, energy, plastic used and wasted, cost per bed). The month-six control test (four binary questions, partial counts as no; the test that lets the ownership claim fail).
- EVERY FIGURE CARRIES ITS LABEL. Verified, workpaper, modelled, target.
