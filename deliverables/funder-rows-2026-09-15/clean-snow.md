# Snow Foundation. The ask.
No form or due date. $100,000 buys 133 Stretch Beds at $750, delivered, facilitation and freight inside the price.

Dear Sally,

Thank you for the catch-up on 1 September. Here is the ask in writing.

The Butterfly Movement Ltd, trading as Goods on Country, asks the Snow Foundation for $100,000 to buy 133 Stretch Beds for families in remote Aboriginal communities who are sleeping on floors.

A Stretch Bed costs $750 delivered. That price covers the recycled-plastic legs pressed from 20 kg of diverted plastic, the washable canvas, the steel poles and hardware, freight to the community, and the paid time of local people who assemble and deliver it. Nothing in this ask is for our running costs.

The beds go to communities we already work in, where a community organisation holds the stock and decides which households receive a bed. That organisation can also sell beds, and the full $750 stays with it. This is how the making and the money move into community hands.

540 beds are already in homes across eleven communities, each recorded against a batch, a community and a date. 320 of those were bought and paid for by four organisations. Forty were pressed at our Queensland facility and assembled at Gamardi, Maningrida, by young people with Homeland School Company this year.

You would receive the asset register extract showing every bed funded, where it went and when, plus the words of the people who made and received them where consent is recorded. We do not claim a health outcome from a bed. We count a person off the floor, a paid hour of work and a kilogram of plastic.

Snow has backed this work since the start. This grant would put the next 133 families on a bed.

Ben Knight
Goods on Country
