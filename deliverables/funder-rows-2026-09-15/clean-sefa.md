# SEFA, Backing the Bold. Expression of interest answers.
Rolling acceptance, Queensland stream. A $150,000 loan that carries the first-year running cost of the organisation, repaid from the $288 each paid bed hands back.

## Organisation name
The Butterfly Movement Ltd, trading as Goods on Country. ACN 155 132 684.

## Organisation type
Charity, registered with the ACNC, DGR endorsed, with a trading arm.

## Legal structure
Company limited by guarantee.

## Where do you operate?
Queensland and the Northern Territory. Production at The Harvest, Witta, Queensland. Beds in eleven communities including Palm Island, Maningrida, Utopia, Tennant Creek and Alice Springs.

## How long have you been operating?
Five years or more. The charity was registered in 2012. The bed work began in 2023 and moved into the charity in 2026.

## Website

## What percentage of your leadership identifies as First Nations?

## Who are your main customers?
Aboriginal charitable trusts, community-controlled health services, homeland education organisations and a university research centre. Four have paid for beds: Centrecorp Foundation, Mala'la Health Service Aboriginal Corporation, Homeland School Company and the ALIVE National Centre at the University of Melbourne. 320 beds, $247,770 net of GST, on five invoices.

## What social issue do you address, who benefits, and how?
In remote communities a bed cannot be bought locally and freight costs more than the bed, so families sleep on the floor. Crowding and floor sleeping drive the skin infections that sit behind rheumatic heart disease. We make a Stretch Bed from 20 kg of recycled plastic, sell it at $750 to organisations that already hold health and housing budgets, and put the making on country so the work and the money stay there. 540 beds are in homes across eleven communities. The households get a bed. The young people who make and deliver the beds get paid work.

## What would the loan enable?
Make and deliver the first 400 beds while the running cost of the organisation is carried, so that every bed sold repays the loan at $288 a bed and the first year does not depend on a grant for the running cost. Working capital against customer invoices, and the yield improvements to the press and cutting line that Nic is quoting, sit inside the same facility.

## Loan amount sought
$150,000. An earlier draft asked for $50,000 to $100,000 in the first instance; that figure is withdrawn and $150,000 is the ask.

## How would the loan be repaid?
From the organisation's share of each bed sold. A bed sells at $750. About $262 makes it, $100 delivers it and $100 pays for the community work around it, leaving about $288 with the organisation. About 124 paid beds a year service $150,000 at the modelled terms and 522 repay it. This year plans 400 beds and four organisations have already bought 320. With no grant at all, 874 paid beds a year carry the full $251,224 running cost. Money that communities earn by selling beds from their own stock stays with them and is never used to repay this loan.

## What does growth look like over the next two to three years?
This year, 400 beds of first stock into community pools and two production plants, if the grants we have applied for land. Then production on country at two sites, with the plants moving to community ownership. Those volumes are targets and no order exists for them yet. In hand today: 320 beds paid for, 40 beds pressed at our own facility and assembled in Maningrida, and a published price anyone can check against our invoices.

## Anything else you would like to tell us?
Nothing is signed today. Every grant in our plan is an application or an invitation. We would rather say that than imply momentum we do not have.
