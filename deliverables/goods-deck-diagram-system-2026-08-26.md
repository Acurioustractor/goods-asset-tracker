# Goods on Country deck diagram system

**Working design brief. 26 August 2026.**

This brief is cut from the live Pencil deck and the latest conversation. It does not settle the
open financial rules. Revenue retention, repayment, inventory ownership and use of proceeds still
need agreement before the model is presented as an operating commitment.

## The visual thesis

The model is not one centrally controlled flywheel.

> **One governed catalyst branches into five locally controlled loops.**

The investor acts once. Goods on Country's Indigenous-led board protects the purpose and agreed
rules. Each community controls the balance between immediate access and local enterprise, then
decides what follows.

This distinction should govern every model diagram:

- shared governance is shown once, before the branch;
- community choice is shown after the branch;
- the five community pathways are related, but not identical;
- giving and selling are two uses of one bed pool, not three rigid program types;
- the return arrow belongs inside each community pathway, not back to the investor;
- unresolved money rules remain visible as a guardrail, not hidden in the illustration.

## Diagram 1: the structural problem

**Deck position:** slide 02.

**Takeaway:** Remote communities import the goods and export the value.

Keep this slide solution-free. The four evidence points should reveal a common cause without
introducing the Stretch Bed or the Goods on Country operating model too early.

Visual structure:

1. Four evidence points: health and housing, community enterprise, paid local work, waste.
2. A single shared statement beneath them: these are costs of an imported supply system.
3. No product image, investor, charity or production facility.

The current slide already has the right narrative function. Improve only if the evidence becomes
hard to read in projection; do not turn it into a problem-solution infographic at this point.

## Diagram 2: product lineage

**Deck position:** slide 04.

**Takeaway:** Each round of community feedback built the next product.

Visual structure:

1. Basket Bed: trial in community and learn what must become simpler and stronger.
2. Pakkimjalki Kari: respond to repeated washing needs; test and refine the one-button machine.
3. Stretch Bed: combine years of bed feedback into a washable, repairable design with
   recycled-plastic frames that can be made closer to community.
4. One method line beneath: listen, prototype, test in real conditions, improve, move making
   closer.

The products must read left to right in chronological order. The visual should feel like one
accumulating line of learning, not three unrelated product cards.

## Diagram 3: governance and decision rights

**Deck position:** slide 07.

**Takeaway:** One shared system. Local decisions stay local.

Visual structure:

1. **Indigenous-led board governs:** purpose, shared assets, appointments and reinvestment.
2. **Goods on Country holds:** the charity, brand, product system, IP, fundraising and shared
   services as the model moves into its long-term home.
3. **Each community decides:** allocation, local sales, local work and when to move toward
   production.

Show these as three layers, not three sequential organisations. The board is the governance layer
of Goods on Country. Community enterprises are independent local decision-makers, not departments
inside the charity.

Status language:

- Indigenous-led today;
- two Indigenous directors appointed;
- stated aim of 100% Indigenous directorship;
- ownership remains a pathway wherever it is not legally complete.

## Diagram 4: the 1,000-bed model

**Deck position:** slide 08.

**Takeaway:** One catalyst starts five community-controlled loops.

Visual structure:

```text
CATALYTIC INVESTOR
funds 1,000 beds
        |
        v
GOODS ON COUNTRY BOARD
governs purpose and agreed rules
        |
        v
FIVE COMMUNITY BED POOLS
5 communities x 200 beds
        |
        v
ONE ENLARGED COMMUNITY LOOP, SHOWN x5
200 beds -> community sets the mix
           /                    \
     give some               sell some
  immediate access      gross sales + paid work
           \                    /
            community decides again
       more beds / youth pathways / local priority / production
                          |
                          +---- returns to that community's loop
```

The diagram should enlarge one representative local loop and mark it **x5**. Drawing five full
loops makes the slide repetitive; drawing only one without the multiplier hides the scale.

The main picture carries the operating logic. A separate metric rail carries exact figures:

- 1,000 durable, washable beds;
- up to $750,000 maximum combined gross sales activity if all beds are sold;
- approximately 20 tonnes of recycled HDPE designed into the frames;
- five community-chosen pathways.

The $750,000 figure is not community income. The 20-tonne figure is a design estimate, not measured
waste diversion.

## Diagram 5: one community, two uses, another decision

**Deck position:** slide 09.

**Takeaway:** Immediate need and enterprise can sit in the same community plan.

Do not draw **give**, **sell** and **both** as three equivalent boxes. "Both" is the adjustable mix
between the other two. Show one 200-bed pool dividing into two streams, then rejoining at the next
community decision.

This is the human-scale explanation of the larger model. It should answer what a community can do,
not what the investor receives.

## Diagram 6: four connected returns

**Deck position:** after the model, not before the solution is earned.

**Takeaway:** One catalyst can activate four connected returns.

Pair each current condition with the bounded opportunity the model creates:

| Current condition | Model opportunity |
| --- | --- |
| 51.3% need at least one extra bedroom | 1,000 washable beds create immediate access; not a housing or clinical outcome claim |
| 3.1% managed their own business | five community-chosen enterprise pathways; not five guaranteed enterprises |
| 38.1% employment in very remote Australia | potential paid roles in assembly, delivery, repair and recycling |
| 275,190t of NT waste went to landfill | approximately 20t recycled HDPE designed into the first 1,000 frames |

This is an impact bridge, not an opening problem slide. It belongs after the audience understands
the governance and community-choice model.

## Diagram 7: the next product loop

**Deck position:** slide 10.

**Takeaway:** The bed is the first product, not the end of the model.

Visual structure:

1. community names the next need;
2. Goods on Country supports design and development;
3. prototype and test in community;
4. train, maintain and build local capability;
5. community decides whether and when production moves closer;
6. the next product enters the local system.

Use the washer as the concrete next proof. Do not imply that every community will build every
piece of machinery.

## The drawing system

- Cream ground, terracotta rule, ink typography and clay/sage accents from the live Pencil deck.
- Playfair Display for takeaway titles; Inter for explanation; Roboto Mono only for short system
  labels and exact stage language.
- Generated Goods illustration only for the human/object metaphor. Exact labels, numbers, arrows
  and caveats remain editable Pencil layers.
- One dominant idea per slide. A single large visual is better than many small icons.
- Do not use a generic circular flywheel when the ownership or cash loop is unresolved.
- Do not use a tree that places communities beneath the charity as controlled subsidiaries.
- Do not use three equal boxes for give, sell and both.
- Do not bake legal, financial or governance claims into generated artwork.

## Recommended build order

1. Slide 08: one catalyst, five local loops.
2. Slide 07: governance and decision rights.
3. Slide 09: one community, two uses, another decision.
4. Move and refine the four-returns impact bridge after the model.
5. Add the learning line to the product lineage only if the current slide still reads as a product
   catalogue when projected.
