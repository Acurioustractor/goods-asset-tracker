Goods on Country · the model, drawn

The Two Loops

Buying something a community already wants is how a community-owned factory gets started.

That is the whole model, and it only works if the money stops in the right place. Catalytic capital pays Goods for a community's first stock. Buyers pay the community enterprise, and that money never reaches Goods. Drawing it as one circle would say the opposite, so it is drawn as two.

The gold arrow is the only crossing, and it carries beds. The red cross is the arrow a funder expects to find and will not. The left loop is what sustains Goods.

The Goods loop

Catalytic capital buys beds at the published $750. Of that, $276 makes the bed and $474 carries the organisation. At 628 paid beds a year that covers the whole $297,550 of running Goods on Country, with no grant support at all.

This is the loop that keeps the lights on, and it closes inside Goods.

The community loop

A community receives 100 beds as first trading stock, already paid for. It sells them to buyers who already hold health and housing budgets, and keeps the whole sale price. A hundred beds at $750 is $75,000 of local capital.

That money funds whatever the community decides next, which may be a plant, may be more stock, may be something we have not thought of.

Why one loop would be a lie

Drawing this as a single circle puts an arrow from community revenue back to catalytic capital, and implies Goods is repaid out of community sales. It is not. The $750 stays with the enterprise, which was ruled on 10 September, and the 50% split that used to sit here is retired.

A funder reading one loop asks the obvious question: if the money comes back, why do you need the grant again next year? The honest answer is that it does not come back, and Goods is carried by its own trade instead. Two loops says that without anybody having to ask.
