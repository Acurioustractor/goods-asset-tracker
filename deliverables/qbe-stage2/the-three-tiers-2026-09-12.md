# The three tiers, drawn

Capital model, enterprise model, impact dashboard. Added to
[The Model](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/051b93d2-24be-4a6b-b6ff-0390889ddff7) on 12 September,
turning three text cards into three drawings. Five SVGs on the page now.

## The capital model

Sources on the left, the four jobs in the middle, the year on the right.

**The arrow in the middle is the one nobody had drawn.** Bed money is asked at the $750 sale price
while the making line is the provisional $276 cost, so $160,000 of bed money over-covers its own
line by **$49,600**. That surplus is the provisional $474 carrying the organisation. Both figures
are provisional while the bought leg panel yield is unknown, and the drawings label them so.

It is also why the arithmetic looks wrong until you see it. The operating line is short $197,550 and
the gap is $147,950. The difference between those two is exactly that arrow.

## The enterprise model

One pool, drawn end to end: 100 beds arrive as trading stock, the enterprise sells them at $750 and
keeps **$75,000**.

| What $75,000 could fund | |
|---|---|
| Half a community plant | One costs $150,000 |
| 271 beds of making | At a provisional $276 each |
| Wages, more stock, something else | Not ours to say |

**And the arithmetic at the end is the catalytic argument.**

> Four pools of 100 beds at $750 is **$300,000**. Two community plants at the $150,000 allowance is
> **$300,000**. The same number QBE is being asked for.

Nobody designed those to match, which is what makes it worth saying. The caption keeps it honest:
it is a statement of scale and not a claim that the money doubles. The beds are funded by Brian M.
Davis, Snow and the gap, and what a community does with its own sales is its decision.

## One rendering bug caught before publishing

The arrowhead marker was defined once in the first SVG and referenced from two others. SVG markers
do not cross between sibling `<svg>` elements, so those arrows would have rendered as plain lines
with no heads. Each SVG now carries its own marker definition.
