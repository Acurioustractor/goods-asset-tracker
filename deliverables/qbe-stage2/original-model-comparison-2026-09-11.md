# What changed between the original cost model and today

`GOC Bed Unit-Costing Model v2 (3).xlsx`, dated 4 August 2026, read against the 9 September running
costs and today's explainer. Seven differences. The last one is the one to read.

---

## 1. Running costs went from $109,500 to $297,550, and one line is most of it

| | Original v2, 4 Aug | Today, 9 Sep | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Founders | $16,800 | $151,200 | **+$134,400** |
| Field travel | $51,000 | $51,000 | unchanged |
| Facility rent, Goods share | $27,000 | $27,000 | unchanged |
| Witta maintenance | not in it | $8,350 | new |
| Admin | $14,700 | not in it | **removed** |
| Consulting and accounting | not in it | $50,000 | **new** |
| Marketing | not in it | $10,000 | **new** |
| **Total** | **$109,500** | **$297,550** | **+$188,050** |

**The founder line changed concept as well as size.** The original charged founders as a *production
lead*: 30 days a year at $560 a day, being the days they actually spend on making. Today's model
carries two full salaries at $75,600 each. That is $134,400 of the $188,050 increase, or 71% of it.

Both can be right. They answer different questions. The original asks what a bed should carry. Today's
asks what the organisation costs. **What cannot happen is one being quoted as if it were the other.**

The other $53,650 is the admin block of $14,700 coming out, and $60,000 of consulting, accounting and
marketing going in. Open Items 15 and 16 in the live sheet are exactly these two moves, and both are
still open.

## 2. The bed did not get cheaper. Freight moved

| | Original, Factory path | Today |
|---|---:|---:|
| HDPE plastic | $55.00 | $55.00 |
| Steel poles | $27.00 | $27.00 |
| Canvas | $93.50 | $93.50 |
| Hardware | $5.24 | $5.24 |
| Diesel | $15.00 | $15.00 |
| Labour | $80.00 | $80.00 |
| **Freight** | **$150.00** | **charged on top** |
| **Total** | **$425.74** | **$275.74** |
| Contribution per bed | $324.26 | $474.26 |

**$425.74 minus $275.74 is exactly $150.** Every other line is identical to the cent. The margin did
not improve by $150; freight became a pass-through. A reader comparing the two without noticing will
think the bed got better.

## 3. $150 freight was in the original all along

The original prices freight at **$150 for the Factory path and $150 for the Community path**, with
$175 for Buy-Kit. Its note reads "Long-haul ~$150/bed (modelled)".

So the $100 that appears in the live sheet's Inputs is a later, lower number, and the $100 against
$150 confusion we spent time on today was never a disagreement in the original model.

## 4. There are three paths, and only one is in the deck

| | Buy-Kit | Factory | Community |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| HDPE plastic | $344.05 | $55.00 | **$0.00** |
| Labour | $40.00 | $80.00 | **$130.00** |
| Freight | $175.00 | $150.00 | $150.00 |
| **Total cost** | **$684.79** | **$425.74** | **$420.74** |
| Contribution | $65.21 | $324.26 | **$329.26** |

**The Community path is the cheapest of the three**, because the plastic is free: community-collected
feedstock enters at $0. It pays labour $130 against Witta's $80 and still comes out ahead.

That is a strong argument for community manufacturing that the deck does not make. It currently
argues from ownership. The model argues from cost as well.

## 5. The facility and the ask were both bigger, and the match was assumed

| | Original v2 | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Facility build | $207,450, charged in full | $150,000 allowance, with $207,450 as the turnkey benchmark |
| Beds a year per factory | 500 | 576 at three a day, 960 at five |
| Capital stack | $250,000 QBE match-eligible + $250,000 assumed 1:1 match + $100,000 other = **$600,000** | $300,000 for two plants, no match |
| Equipment maintenance | 5% of capex a year | Open Item 13, still an estimate |

**The $250,000 ask and the 1:1 match are both retired.** Ruling V established QBE is catalytic and
not dollar for dollar, and the ask is now $300,000 for two plants. The original's whole capital
stack rests on a match that does not exist.

## 6. The value ladder, priced from invoices Goods already pays

The original prices each rung at what **Goods already pays Defy today**, from named invoices.

| Step | What the community ends up holding | Worth per bed |
|---|---|---:|
| Collection and baling | Sorted, caged HDPE ready to shred | no price exists |
| Shredding | HDPE shred | $40.00 |
| Pressing and CNC | Finished HDPE leg kit, cut and edged | $344.05 |
| Assembly | A finished bed, ready to go out | $400.00 |
| Sales and delivery | Beds in homes, sold by the community | $750.00 |

Its own line: *"this money goes to Sydney now, and we would rather it went to the community doing the
work."* And: **shred to pressed kits multiplies what a bed is worth to a community by 8.6 times**,
from $40 to $344.05.

This is the clearest statement of the model anywhere, and it is not in the deck or the explainer.

## 7. The finding that should change the pitch

The original models five pathways for a community, at 450 beds' worth of material a year.

| Option | Setup | Earns a year | Running a year | Left over |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Collect and shred, today's ask | $56,600 to $103,300 | $18,000 | $51,043 | **−$33,043** |
| Collect, shred and sell | $56,600 to $103,300 | $175,500 | $52,843 | **+$122,657** |
| Collect, shred and press | $89,380 to $136,080 | $154,823 | $71,081 | **+$83,742** |
| Sell and deliver only, no plant | $31,800 to $64,000 | $157,500 | $36,800 | **+$120,700** |
| The whole chain | $95,767 to $142,467 | $337,500 | $79,333 | **+$258,167** |

**Every pathway that includes selling makes money. The only one that loses money is the one without
it.** Collect-and-shred, which the model itself labels "today's ask", loses $33,043 a year, because
the site floor lands the moment anyone works there at all.

The model's own words: *"selling is worth MORE than pressing and needs NO extra capex, because the
site base is already there."* And it names what it does not know: *"FREIGHT COMES OUT OF THAT AND IS
NOT MODELLED, right shape, unproven size."*

**What this means for the QBE pitch.** The whole chain earns the most and roughly doubles the return
for roughly double the setup, so the plant earns its place. What it does not do is make the money. Selling does. A pitch that says the plant is the thing will be argued with by its own model.

## 8. The network fee, which the deck has never used

The shared team costs **$109,500 a year whether there is one site or five.** So:

| Sites in the network | Shared cost per site |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | $109,500 |
| 2 | $54,750 |
| 3 | $36,500 |
| 4 | $27,375 |

Its heading: **"why the third site is in the FIRST community's interest."** That is an argument for
funding two plants at once that nobody has made, and it comes with its own caveat in the original:
"NOT AGREED WITH ANY COMMUNITY, this is the arithmetic, not an offer."

Note the shared team is costed at $109,500, the original overhead. At today's $297,550 the per-site
figures roughly triple.

---

## What the original refuses to say, and we should keep refusing

- It never splits money arriving at a community into wages and surplus. That is the community's
  decision, and the model says putting a number there "repeats exactly the mistake this tab
  replaces: putting a number where a conversation belongs."
- It does not cost bought-in feedstock for a pathway starting partway down the chain.
- It does not price a site base for a community that asked for no production modules.
- Its banner: **"NOTHING HERE HAS BEEN OFFERED TO ANY COMMUNITY. No community sees a price for their
  own pathway before they have been walked through it in person."**
