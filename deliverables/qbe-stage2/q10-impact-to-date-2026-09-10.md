Beds are counted in a register, one row per bed, with an asset identifier, a community and a
product. The register is the source of every count below and it is the same record we use to find a
bed again when someone reports a fault.

### Why 540 and 320 are both true

The register counts beds that reached a community, including prototypes, gifted beds and the
Basket Beds that came before the current product. The invoice record counts beds someone paid us
for. A bed can sit in one, the other or both.

The 130 Centrecorp beds are the reason we do not simply add the two. Their invoice, their delivery
record and their register rows have not been reconciled against each other yet, so we hold them
outside any cumulative total, where they cannot be counted twice. When that reconciliation is done we
will report the number it produces, including if it is lower than 540.

### How each figure is graded

Every figure we publish carries one of four labels, and the label travels with the number.

- **Verified** means we read the source. The 540, the 11 communities and the ten invoices are
  verified.
- **Workpaper** means it comes from a reconciliation we did ourselves and can hand over.
- **Modelled** means it is calculated. Two hours of paid making a bed and 20 kilograms of plastic
  in a finished bed are both modelled.
- **Target** means it is a plan.

### What we hear, held separately from what we count

Experience is recorded in Empathy Ledger, our consent-based storytelling platform, with consent
held per person and per quote. A repo registry governs what may be printed and it is the record
that wins. Dorrie Jones at Arlparra said of her bed, "Good for me and comfy, easy to put together."
Ray Nelson, against bed GBO-156-96, reported that the household is no longer experiencing back
pains. Those are reported experience. They are not a measured health outcome and we do not present
them as one.

### What we do not claim

No clinical result. Scabies and rheumatic heart disease are why a bed is washable and off the
ground, and that is where the claim stops. We have never run a health study and we do not infer one
from a delivery.

Three things are not measured yet and we would rather name them than imply them. Paid hours a bed
at working pace, which the first fifty beds out of each new plant will settle. Weighed yield, which
needs input, accepted parts, offcuts and waste weighed per batch. And household use over time,
which needs a consented follow-up at delivery, six weeks and three months.
