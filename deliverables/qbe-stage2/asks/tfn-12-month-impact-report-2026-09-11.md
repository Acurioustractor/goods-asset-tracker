### 7. How the funding was used and the project implemented, in words

The money built the facility.

We put a hydraulic press, a computer-controlled router and a plastic shredder into a shed at Witta
in Queensland and started turning waste plastic into bed parts. The press runs at 180°C. Each bed
takes 36 kg of shredded plastic through it and carries 20 kg in the finished product.

The proof that it works is Maningrida. Forty beds were pressed end to end at our facility, freighted
to Arnhem Land and assembled at Gamardi by local workers, and bought by the Homeland School Company
in May 2026. Before the facility we bought parts in. After it, we made them.

Since then every Stretch Bed has been routed and assembled at this facility. 177 Stretch Beds are
now deployed across eleven communities, alongside 363 Basket Beds from the earlier design, and
22 washing machines.

The facility is also the thing that makes the next step possible. Because we now know what it costs
to make a bed and how long it takes, we can build the same plant in a community and hand it over.
Two community plants are the subject of our current application to the QBE Foundation, and a third
is approved for Alice Springs through Oonchiumpa.
