## Slide A: what people have already paid for

**Four organisations have paid us for 320 beds.**
Centrecorp Foundation, Mala'la Health Service, Homeland School Company and the ALIVE National
Centre. Five invoices, every one settled. They paid $273,966, which is $247,770 of trade once
GST comes out.

**The price a buyer pays has gone from $370 to $800.**
Centrecorp paid $370 a bed for the first sixty in August 2025, and those sixty were Basket Beds,
the first prototype, so that price does not describe what Goods sells today. ALIVE paid $800 each
for a hundred Stretch Beds on INV-0342, settled 20 August 2026. The list price is $750.

**Centrecorp bought and paid for 167 beds across two invoices.**
147 of them are in households and 20 are made and waiting at Alice Springs. The 130 on quote
QU-0014 from May 2026 was a quote and was never paid. Nothing in the register or the ledger is 130.

**They paid $50,000 for the making as well as the beds.**
Ten community-build and program-support visits at $3,000, $6,000 and $8,000, settled with the bed
lines on the same invoices.

**The forty Maningrida beds were pressed at our plant and put together at Gamardi**
with young people and Homeland School Company. Homeland paid $750 a bed, $5,900 of freight and
$8,000 for the trip, and a Goods in Kind Partnership line took $14,190 off that invoice before it
was paid.

**One invoice to a buyer on this list is unpaid.**
INV-0341 is $66,000 including GST for a twelve-month storytelling program with ALIVE. It was
authorised and is past its due date of 30 July 2026. No beds sit on it, and ALIVE has paid in full
for its hundred.

## Slide B: who has asked, and what they did

**We hold no demand total and we do not claim one.**
A total would add a figure scoped to one Elder's wallet to a figure scoped to the children of an
outstation to a figure nobody wrote a scope for at all. Five communities have asked us for beds
and we can name who asked in every case, so the record is a ladder of acts. Strongest first.

**Money moved. Four organisations, 320 beds, $273,966.**
Five settled invoices. The test is whether an invoice settled, and this is the only rung that
also proves the price.

**Money named. Dianne Stokes, twenty beds, Tennant Creek.**
The Warumungu and Warlmanpa Elder who named the Pakkimjalki Kari washing machines in Warumungu
offered to self-fund twenty. No invoice has been raised and no date has been set. She has thought about the
cost, which most people who ask have not.

**An organisation asked. Utopia 150, Homeland Schools Co. 65, Palm Island Community Company 40.**
Utopia gave a rule with its figure: beds for every child. Homeland Schools Co. asked for the kids
in the Maningrida homelands, and the same operator has already paid for forty beds at the list
price. The Palm Island figure came in a partner update with no rule and no scope behind it. A body
with authority asking is evidence about the relationship, and it says nothing about how many beds.

**A person asked. Norman Frank, three beds, Tennant Creek.**
He asked for three in the maroon colourway. Somebody who has chosen a colour is further along than
an enquiry about five hundred.

**Raised in a meeting. Groote Archipelago, 500 beds.**
Simone Grimmond at WHSAC raised five hundred beds and three hundred washing machines in one
meeting. Nothing was quoted, no order was discussed, and the record is logged as exploring. This
is the largest figure we hold and the weakest evidence we hold, and both of those belong on the
same line.

**Turning a conversation into an order is a trip to a community.**
Buyers have already paid us $50,000 to do that work.

**What would settle any of this is a count.**
Four questions per household, asked by a local person who is paid for it: how many sleep here,
how many have a bed, how many are on the floor, who would use a new bed first. Until one community
does that, every need figure we hold is an impression, including the good ones.
