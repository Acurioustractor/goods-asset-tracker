# Review of the ChatGPT QBE pitch session

Read 11 September 2026. Three things to adopt, three to correct, one that breaks a standing rule.

## What is right, and better than what we had

**Drawing it as a loop.** Correct instinct, and it matches deck slide 08, "One catalyst starts a
loop a community controls."

**"Procurement shifted" as the number in the middle.** This is the best idea in the session and it
is new. Institutional dollars that now land in a community enterprise instead of a southern
supplier. It names the counterfactual, which "money kept in community" does not: that money was
going to be spent anyway, somewhere else. It is countable per sale and nobody is counting it.

**Adopt it as the fifth measure**, sitting above the four areas. It is the one that makes the model
catalytic instead of charitable, so it belongs in the middle of the drawing.

**The three-tier split.** Capital model, enterprise model, impact dashboard. That is close to what
the modules already hold, and the naming is better than ours.

## What is wrong

**Suncorp is not a buyer.** The session names "the home school company and Suncorp and the Alive
Foundation" as identified institutional buyers. Suncorp appears nowhere in any record we hold. It
is almost certainly Centrecorp, misheard by the voice model. Our four paid buyers are Centrecorp,
ALIVE, Homeland School Company and Mala'la Health Service. **Naming Suncorp on a slide would be a
fabricated claim about a listed company.**

**ALIVE is not a foundation.** It is the ALIVE National Centre at the University of Melbourne.

**The raise is not $500,000 to $600,000.** $600,000 is asked across five lines. The year needs
$747,950 and the gap is $147,950. The $500,000 low end understates the ask by up to $148,000.

## What breaks a standing rule

**Health cannot be a dial.** The session proposes four scorecards, "employment, enterprise,
recycling, health," as "simple dials." Health is the reason the work exists and is never a claimed
outcome. It keeps its place as one of the four areas and it carries no number.

In the drawing it reads: **the reason we started.** No dial, no score.

## The correction that matters most: there are two loops

The session draws a single circular flow, ending "back to local production, and back around to next
product." A single circle puts an arrow from community revenue back to catalytic capital and implies
Goods is repaid out of community sales.

**It is not.** The full $750 stays with the enterprise. Ben ruled that on 10 September and the 50%
split that used to sit there is retired. Ruling 9 already says it: *the two kinds of money that never
meet.*

A funder reading one loop asks the obvious question. If the money comes back, why do you need the
grant again next year? The honest answer is that it does not come back, and Goods is carried by its
own trade, at 628 paid beds a year.

**Two loops answers that without anybody having to ask.** The drawing is at
`pages/two-loops.html` and published at
https://claude.ai/code/artifact/58d96a4b-616e-49ac-b274-8237929ae68a

- **The Goods loop.** Catalytic capital, made at Witta, Goods paid $750 a bed, $474 carries the
  business, the next bed and the next plant.
- **The community loop.** First stock arrives, institutional buyers pay the enterprise, local
  capital of $75,000 a pool, local production, next product.
- **One crossing, in gold, and it carries beds.**
- **One deliberate break, in red**, where a funder expects an arrow and will not find one.

## One thing the session got right that I doubted

The session reads the model as four community nodes of 100 beds each, totalling 400. I thought that
inflated a single 100-bed pool by 300. It does not. **Ruling 7 is four 100-bed pools**, one each in
Tennant Creek, Utopia, Palm Island and Maningrida, at $10,000 of facilitation each. Four pools of
100 is 400 beds and $40,000 of facilitation, which is exactly the first-stock line and exactly the
facilitation line. The numbers tie.
