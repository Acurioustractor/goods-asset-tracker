# What $300,000 produces

**Goods on Country · The Butterfly Movement Ltd · QBE Foundation Stage 2, question 9 · September 2026**

This document covers the impact of the funds requested. It does not restate the organisation.
The model, the money and the evidence behind every figure sit in the materials attached at
question 23.

---

## What the money builds

Two production plants, one each in a community that has asked for one, at an allowance of
$150,000 a plant. A plant is a site base and four modules: collection and sorting, shredding,
pressing with CNC and finishing, and assembly. It takes about three months from money to first
bed. It starts at 200 beds a year on one press and reaches 720 on the same equipment as the local
crew builds speed.

The two we plan to build are Palm Island, first, and Maningrida. Both have asked us for a plant.
Alice Springs has asked as well and is funded separately through Oonchiumpa's Commonwealth
funding, so it is outside this request.

## What each bed does, with the unit it is counted in

| Outcome | Unit | Per bed |
|---|---|---|
| Paid making | hours | 2 |
| Plastic kept in use | kilograms of HDPE | 20 |
| Money kept in the community | dollars, when the community sells the bed | up to 750 |
| A person off the floor | one bed, one person | 1 |

Health is the reason the hardware exists and it is not one of these rows. Scabies and rheumatic
heart disease are why a bed is washable and off the ground. That is where the claim stops.

## What two plants produce in their first year

At 200 beds a plant in year one, 400 beds across the two sites.

| | Year one, both plants |
|---|---|
| Beds made in community | 400 |
| Paid making | 800 hours |
| Plastic kept in use | 8 tonnes |
| People off the floor | 400 |

The money row is deliberately absent from that table. What share of a bed's price stays with the
community enterprise is a decision each community makes with us, and none of them has made it
yet. No community's own pathway gets a price in a document before they have seen it.

## How each row is counted

Beds are units in a live register, counted per community, and the register is the source of every
count we publish. Every figure we print carries a label: verified, workpaper, modelled or target.
The two rows above that are modelled today are hours and kilograms, at 2 hours and 20 kilograms a
bed. The first fifty beds out of each plant are weighed and timed at the site, which replaces both
with measured numbers.

Voices are held in Empathy Ledger, our consent-based storytelling platform, with consent recorded
per person and per quote. The rule that joins the two records is that no number goes out without a
voice and no voice is reduced to a number.

## The test at month six

Six months after a plant starts, we ask four questions at the site. Each has a yes or a no.

1. Who holds the keys.
2. Who runs the payroll.
3. Who invoices the buyer.
4. Whether at least half the production is local.

We report the answers whichever way they fall. A plant that answers no to three of them is a
finding, and it is more useful to us and to you than a plant that is reported as a success.

---

## What this grant does not pay for, and why that matters

It does not buy beds. The first stock is paid for separately, so that a community's plant and a
community's stock never compete for the same dollar. A plant can then be judged on whether it
runs.

It does not pay for the youth programme wrapped around a site. In Alice Springs that is the
Commonwealth's. Elsewhere it is $10,000 of facilitation a community, which sits in a separate
application.

---

## What happens after the two plants

Nobody will lend against a community-made bed today, because nobody knows what one costs to make
in a community. Our costs come from a shed in Witta.

These two plants produce that number in their first year, along with the operating cost of running
a plant a long way from a city. Our own test says a $200,000 loan needs 101 paid beds a year to
service it, which is inside a single plant's output. Grant capital builds plants one and two.
A measured cost is what lets repayable capital build plants three and four.
