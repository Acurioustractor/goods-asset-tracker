## 2. What the $750,000 is

> Goods on Country is raising $750,000 to put 1,000 beds into five communities, with each
> community deciding what is given, what is sold, who is paid and what comes next.

One thousand Stretch Beds at the public price of $750 (verified against the live products table).
On today's kit path a delivered bed costs about $685 (workpaper), so the pool leaves roughly $65 a
bed and funds none of the network block: $109,500 a year at minimum (workpaper), about $300,000 a
year as the public ask. Two pots stays true. Bed money makes beds. Philanthropy buys the block.

It is catalytic in the sense `CONTEXT.md` now defines: money for a useful first step that leaves a
community with more control of the work, tested by what remains after the first spend. After this
money is spent, five communities hold beds, paid assembly and delivery work, sales money where
they chose to sell, and the right to decide the next step.

Open by design, and said on every surface until it is closed: "The rules for sales money, resale
and who holds the beds are being agreed with each community. Until they are, the numbers are a
design, not a promise."

### 3.2 The recommended stack

Keep the pool arithmetic and make two changes.

**TFFF funds the block, not the beds.** $100,000 a year for three years is almost exactly the
minimum network block, and "the resilience of organisations doing good work" is block language.
This is the one move that funds the block, which every earlier version of this raise left
unfunded, and it gives TFFF an application written in their strategy's own words.

**QBE's $400,000 is 400 beds plus the proof layer, not 533 beds.** Two pools (400 beds, $300,000)
plus $100,000 for the things bed money never funds: the measured production run inside the first
pool, five community rules agreements, and the evidence and finance work that turns the run into
proof a lender can read.

No community is named against a pool in this table, and none will be until it has seen its pool.

If Ben keeps TFFF's first year inside the bed pool instead, the pool reaches 1,000 and the block is

## 4. The QBE application, in the order the form asks

**Amount (Q5): $250,000** (Ben, 2 September, evening; this replaces the $400,000 written earlier
in the day). One governed pool (200 beds, $150,000) and the proof block ($100,000: the measured
run, the rules agreements, traceability and the accounting repair). It does not have to be the
maximum. It is inside their range, above the 2025 average, easier to say and easier to grant,
and every dollar any funder adds buys beds at the same ratio. $400,000 stays the ceiling and is
never presented as the plan (ruling V).

**Smaller amount (Q7): $150,000.** The proof block ($75,000) and the first hundred beds. The loop
is proven on half a pool; the second half waits for the next funder.

**The unit, so any amount reads the same way.** One bed at $750 does four things: a bed off the
ground (verified); 20kg of recycled plastic, so fifty beds is one tonne (workpaper); about 6.5
hours of local work when made locally (modelled, 3.5 of them CNC, the measured run decides) and
about $130 of fair-wage labour in the community cost state (modelled); and up to $750 that stays
local if the community sells it (target). `bed-ratio.ts` holds the ratio; slide 10C draws it.

| Amount | Beds | Pools | Plastic | Local work, modelled | Stays local if all sold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $150,000 | 200 | 1 | 4t | 1,300 hours | up to $150,000 |
| $250,000 | 333 | 1.7 | 6.7t | 2,170 hours | up to $250,000 |
| $400,000 | 533 | 2.7 | 10.7t | 3,470 hours | up to $400,000 |
| $750,000 | 1,000 | 5 | 20t | 6,500 hours | up to $750,000 |

**Without this grant, said plainly.** Every other dollar in the stack buys beds or keeps the
organisation standing. A catalytic grant is the only money that buys the proof: the first fifty
beds pressed and timed, and the rules each pool runs on. Without it the pools are a gift. With
it they are the first step of an enterprise, and the equipment loan behind them can be written.

**Use of funds (Q6).**

- 400 Stretch Beds into two community pools under agreed local rules.
- The measured run: the first fifty of those beds pressed at the farm at production rate, timed
  and costed with receipts. This converts the modelled $426 pressed-path cost into a measured one,
  the single input every lender has asked for and nobody has.
- Five community agreements on allocation, sales money, resale, paid work and stock, so the pools
  are governed before beds move.
- Product traceability for the 1,000 beds, so kilograms per bed become a measurement (ruling T),
  and the accounting repair that gives Goods on Country a gross margin on paper.

**Impact to date (Q10).** Canon: 540 beds, 11 communities, nine years, 40 beds pressed end to end
and built at Gamardi with Homeland School Company. Measured through the register and Empathy
Ledger. The claim ceiling holds: scabies to rheumatic heart disease is the reason the hardware
matters, never a claimed outcome.

**Catalytic effect (Q14, Q18).** Leverage is the metric QBE publishes (3.7x in 2025). State it as
a chain with a release condition on every link, never as a total.

1. QBE funds two pools and the proofs.
2. That work produces community agreements, a measured cost, buyer paper and a governed pool.
3. Alongside it, three invitations already in hand: TFFF $300K for the block, BMDF $100K for pool
   three, Snow's letter for pool four. Minderoo and Dusseldorp in conversation. **$0 signed
   today**, stated first.
4. After the measured run: SEFA $300K and White Box $150K for equipment and working capital, which
   cannot proceed today because the cost is modelled and the borrower is unsettled.
5. Demand already paying: ALIVE's 100 beds, Centrecorp's 130-bed quote deferred pending community
   feedback, and more than 200 requests in each of Tennant Creek and Mparntwe.

QBE is never described as doubling, triggering or guaranteeing anything.

## 5. The entity

Ruling X moved the whole model into Goods on Country, a business name of The Butterfly Movement
Ltd (ABN 22 155 132 684, DGR since January 2012, an Australian public company, so it passes the
"Australian-incorporated" test). TFFF invited Butterfly. BMDF invited Goods On Country. Snow's
grants land in Butterfly. Every external dollar in §3 lands in Butterfly. The cohort entrant is A
Curious Tractor Pty Ltd, now the historic maker in transition.

**The route, ruled 5 September (Ben, ruling AA).** The Butterfly Movement Ltd applies and receives
the grant. A Curious Tractor is listed as the related entity: cohort entrant, holder of the
historic trading record, transferring assets under ruling X. Nicholas Marchesi, sole trader, is
listed as the historic trading vehicle whose books carry FY26. The structure diagram is three
boxes and two arrows, sole trader to A Curious Tractor to Butterfly, with the transfer dated. Q8
states that all funded activity sits in Butterfly and that A Curious Tractor delivers nothing under
the grant after transfer.

**Not the route (ruling AA), recorded and closed.** A Curious Tractor applies and receives the grant,
the external commitments land in Butterfly, and Q2 and Q8 rest on the inter-entity agreement,
which is unsigned. That route needs MinterEllison and a signature before 13 November. It is weaker
and the application should say so rather than hide it.

Either way, "not a charity simply seeking donations" is answered by the trading facts: $750 beds
sold to buyers, ALIVE's paid order, the Centrecorp quote, and a repayable equipment raise that
follows the measured run.
