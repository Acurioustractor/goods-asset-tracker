# NT plastics + overcrowding: the verified facts (2026-08-24)

Every figure below was checked against the primary document on 2026-08-24 (PDFs downloaded
and text-extracted where possible). One widely-circulated figure failed verification and is
corrected here. Honesty labels follow `cost-story.ts` conventions.

Caveats: the 4,933 t is plastic **recycled**, not collected — more plastic sits inside the
275,190 t landfill stream. The WRINT report is industry-commissioned, not an NT EPA
publication. Usable HDPE/PP after polymer separation, contamination and existing contracts
is a fraction of any of these numbers.

## The supply fact (the paragraph these numbers earn)

The NT recycled 4,933 tonnes of plastic last year, and more again went to landfill.
A Stretch Bed's pressed legs take about 20 kg of it [canon; Ben ruling 2026-08-24,
DECISIONS.md T], so one tonne of clean HDPE is roughly forty to fifty beds' legs, and the
Census counts 2,761 very-remote NT households short at least a bedroom. Feedstock will
never be the constraint. What the money buys is the part that is: a community that can
collect the plastic, make the goods, and come to own the making.

**Never use:** "~800 t/yr CDS plastic" (unsupported — real figure ~530 t PET) ·
"45 kg of plastic per bed" and everything derived from it (22 beds/t, 17,700 beds,
109,600 beds) — canon is ~20 kg, legs only, and beds-per-tonne stays modelled until
the measured run weighs it.
