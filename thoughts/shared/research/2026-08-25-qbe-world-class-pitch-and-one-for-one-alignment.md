# QBE pitch research: a world-class 10-slide spine and the one-for-one distinction

**Date:** 25 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Ground the next reusable Goods deck in current QBE/Social Impact Hub priorities, first-party pitch guidance and the corrected community/donor one-for-one concept.  
**Status:** Research and deck implications, not an approved funding model or final deck.

## Bottom line

The deck needs one proposition, not ten topics:

> Back the next demand-led bed program so essential household goods create paid local work and a practical pathway for communities to hold more of the making.

History, products and the production facility are evidence for that proposition. They are not three separate stories competing with the ask.

The most important distinction is:

1. **Community/donor one-for-one** is a proposed bed purchasing mechanism.
2. **QBE Catalysing Impact** is a separate catalytic-capital program linked to external capital commitments.
3. **Repayable finance** is a third mechanism for productive assets and working capital with a credible repayment source.

QBE must never be presented as the donor that automatically matches each bed, and a $400,000 QBE grant must never be presented as guaranteed.

## What QBE and Social Impact Hub actually reward

### Verified from current primary sources

The current Social Impact Hub program page says strong applications:

- align clearly with climate resilience and/or inclusion;
- articulate a credible theory of change and impact model;
- demonstrate traction and a realistic growth plan;
- show readiness to raise more funding and use the grant as leverage; and
- show enthusiasm for diagnostics, advisory, mentoring and QBE engagement.

The program targets Australian-incorporated social enterprises seeking investment or repayable finance. It explicitly says it is not designed for a charity simply seeking donations. Stage 2 is for enterprises that show strong progress and fundraising traction. Grants are typically AU$150,000–AU$400,000 and linked to external commitments already secured. SIH explicitly says the grants are catalytic **rather than simply matching funds**. [Social Impact Hub: Catalysing Impact](https://www.socialimpacthub.org/catalysing-impact)

The official 2026 cohort announcement names **A Curious Tractor** as one of ten enterprises and describes the cohort's models as grounded in community need and designed to grow. It states that the 2026 program offers up to AU$1.1 million across the cohort. In 2025, AU$1.02 million of QBE grants sat within an AU$3.77 million total capital stack, including AU$2.75 million in external funding commitments. [Social Impact Hub: 2026 cohort announcement](https://www.socialimpacthub.org/news/social-impact-hub-and-qbe-foundation-announce-the-2026-catalysing-impact-cohort-and-up-to-11m-in-grants)

QBE says it seeks ventures that combine **commercial viability with measurable impact**. It describes Catalysing Impact as building investment-ready enterprises and using QBE funding to help secure third-party investment that might not otherwise be possible. [QBE 2025 Impact Report, p.13](https://www.qbe.com/media/qbe/group/sustainability/2026/qbe-2025-impact-report.pdf)

The QBE launch release also emphasises climate resilience, inclusion, third-party investment, operating scale and measurable social and environmental contribution. Its 2025 phrase “matched grants” is older than the current 2026 SIH explanation that the grants are catalytic rather than simple matching. Use the current wording for the 2026 submission. [QBE launch release, 22 January 2025](https://www.qbe.com/newsroom/news/qbe-foundation-catalysing-impact-initiative)

SIH's investment-readiness diagnostic covers scaling, social objective, impact measurement, business model and cost structure, strategy, financial model, people, legal structure, investment terms and investor approach. A ten-slide deck should show the logic and strongest evidence; the evidence pack should carry the full diligence. [Social Impact Hub: Impact Investment Readiness Diagnostic](https://www.socialimpacthub.org/initiative/impact-investment-readiness-diagnostic-tool)

### Goods-specific Stage 2 requirements

Jay Boolkin's July 2026 emails require the amount, use of funds, expected impact, verifiable funder commitments, deployment speed, existing pitch materials, repayment history, theory of change or impact evidence, full financials, governance documents, entity structure, and an audit or accountant's letter. The sourced internal record is [`wiki/investor/19-qbe-stage2-application-requirements.md`](../../../wiki/investor/19-qbe-stage2-application-requirements.md).

Two implications follow:

- This should be a reusable external capital deck, not a decorative QBE-only submission. Jay explicitly asked for pitch material already used externally.
- The slide deck cannot repair diligence gaps. Entity clarity, financials, commitment evidence and governance belong in the supporting pack.

## What first-party pitch guidance adds

Sequoia says the quality of a pitch comes from clarity of thought and ambition, not the slides themselves. Its canonical structure begins with a one-sentence purpose, then problem, solution, why now, market, alternatives, business model, team, financials and long-term vision. [Sequoia: Writing a Business Plan](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan)

Sequoia's presentation guidance says the first five minutes must earn the rest of the audience's attention. It recommends opening with what changed, a one-sentence explanation of what the company does, and a small set of fast facts. It also recommends simple financials that connect use of money to specific milestones, completing the presentation in about 20 minutes and preserving time for discussion. [Sequoia: How to Present to Investors](https://articles.sequoiacap.com/how-to-present-to-investors)

The Funding Network is directly relevant because the current Catalysing Impact program names a live employee crowdfunding event delivered with TFN. TFN pitch coach Emily Carr advises organisations to choose one message because trying to cover every program becomes confusing, and to make the call to action concise and specific. TFN also emphasises personal storytelling, authenticity and a unique case for support. [The Funding Network: Fundraise Better with Pitch Coaching](https://www.thefundingnetwork.com.au/post/fundraise-better-with-pitch-coaching/) and [TFN workshops](https://www.thefundingnetwork.com.au/non-profit-workshops/)

### Applied to Goods

- **One decision:** support the next demand-led production and delivery program.
- **One narrative:** the road taught Goods that delivering products is not enough; more of the making must move closer to community control.
- **One program:** the next beds create household hardware, paid local work and evidence for the next production step.
- **One precise ask:** state the amount, instrument, use, timing and measurable milestone. Do not end with a menu of four unrelated requests.
- **One visual job per slide:** a person, product, process, proof point or funding mechanism, not a collage of all five.

## Corrected one-for-one model

### What the supplied conversation supports

The transcript says:

> If a community buys one, we match it with a donor, while keeping the bed price at $750.

If **1,000 is the total number of beds delivered**, the clean arithmetic is:

| Source | Beds | Funding |
|---|---:|---:|
| Community purchasers | 500 | AU$375,000 |
| Bed-matching philanthropy | 500 | AU$375,000 |
| **Total** | **1,000** | **AU$750,000** |

This is **500 purchased beds plus 500 matched beds**, not a 50% discount on 1,000 independently purchased beds. It also is not evidence that QBE will provide the AU$375,000 donor pool.

The strongest framing is:

> **Match demonstrated community demand.** Every community-funded bed activates a second bed under agreed community allocation rules.

This avoids turning population-based need into an order and gives philanthropy a clear leverage story.

### What remains unverified

The conversation does **not** settle:

- whether 1,000 means total beds or 1,000 purchased plus 1,000 matched;
- whether communities may resell matched beds;
- whether sale proceeds may fund any community priority or must stay in the bed/production program;
- who owns inventory, warranty and bad-debt risk;
- which community organisations have authorised an order and quantity;
- whether delivery, youth wages and local program costs sit inside or outside the AU$750 price.

The list of possible communities in the conversation is brainstorming, not authorised demand. The resale/community-profit extension is a promising design option, not yet a fact. It needs explicit agreement with community purchasers and the bed-matching funder before it appears as a promise.

## Keep three funding mechanisms visually separate

| Mechanism | Job | Honest deck language |
|---|---|---|
| Community purchase + bed-matching philanthropy | Pays full price for a demand-led bed run | “For every community-funded bed, a donor funds another, subject to agreed allocation rules.” |
| QBE catalytic grant | Helps bring external capital into the enterprise and de-risk growth | “A discretionary Catalysing Impact grant linked to external commitments”; never “QBE doubles every dollar.” |
| Repayable finance | Funds productive equipment and/or working capital | Name the asset, borrower, repayment source, term and release milestone. |

The deck can show how the mechanisms reinforce each other, but it should not blend them into one “match”.

## Research-informed 10-slide logic

These are functional titles, not final copy:

1. **The ambition** — one declarative sentence: communities progressively hold more of the making.
2. **Why household hardware matters** — one community voice and the preventable problem.
3. **Nine years on the road** — the few moments that changed the model, not a complete chronology.
4. **Products built for remote life** — Stretch Bed, washing machine prototype and open-source Basket Bed, with status clear.
5. **What has been proven** — verified delivery/product traction and the Maningrida production run; uncertainties named.
6. **From plastic to finished bed** — facility process and why moving steps closer to community matters.
7. **The next 1,000 beds** — the single next program: demand-led beds plus paid local assembly and support.
8. **Buy one. Match one. Build what comes next.** — show the bed arithmetic and community allocation choices, marked proposed until rules are agreed.
9. **Capital with three jobs** — bed revenue, QBE catalytic grant and repayable finance, each tied to a milestone and legal home.
10. **The decision** — one amount and instrument for this audience, what it achieves, by when, and the next action.

This sequence preserves the Goods strategy that the road leads and the model is earned, while answering the QBE scorecard by the end: community need, inclusion, traction, realistic growth, commercial viability, measurable impact, investment readiness and leverage.

## Content and visual rules for the build

- Write slide titles as conclusions, not labels. “Forty beds proved the full process” is stronger than “Traction”.
- Let one approved community voice carry the problem and one carry the possibility. Do not use people as decoration.
- Use real product and making photographs. Avoid stock imagery and dense icon systems.
- Put no more than one equation on the one-for-one slide.
- Mark `verified`, `modelled`, `proposed` and `open` claims visibly in presenter notes or footnotes.
- Do not claim health outcomes. Show access to appropriate health hardware as the immediate output and scabies/RHD as the reason the hardware matters.
- Use economic participation and First Nations opportunity as the strongest QBE inclusion alignment; circular material and transition value are a second strand.
- Name **Goods.** as the enterprise, while the ask/footer clearly states that the QBE cohort entrant and relevant legal applicant is **A Curious Tractor**. Explain Goods on Country/The Butterfly Movement only where the charity-funded wraparound role matters.
- Keep the detail on products, governance, financials, evidence and community pathways in an appendix/data room. Ten slides earn the diligence conversation; they are not the diligence room.

## Claims that should not enter the deck yet

- “QBE will match the 1,000-bed program.”
- “We will receive AU$400,000 from QBE.”
- “These named communities have ordered 1,000 beds.”
- “A community can resell the donor-funded bed and use the profit for anything.”
- “One thousand beds is a measured year of facility production.”
- “Community ownership is already complete.”
- Any youth employment count, wage or job outcome without a funded delivery model and accountable local partner.
