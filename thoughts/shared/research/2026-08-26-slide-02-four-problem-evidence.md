# Slide 02 — four problem statements and evidence

Research date: 26 August 2026. Primary official sources only.

## Recommended slide treatment

### 1. HEALTH — Crowded homes compound preventable disease

**Hero data:** **51.3%**

**Slide copy:** In very remote NT, 51.3% of First Nations households needed at least one extra bedroom in 2021. The NT also had Australia's highest recorded ARF/RHD prevalence in 2024.

**Evidence and definitions**

- The 2021 Census counted 2,761 of 5,377 occupied private dwellings where at least one resident was Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander in **Very Remote Australia (NT)** as needing one or more extra bedrooms: **51.3%**. This is the ABS housing-utilisation measure and is the cleanest defensible overcrowding statistic for the geography Goods serves. [ABS 2021 First Nations QuickStats — Very Remote Australia (NT)](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/IQSRA74)
- At 31 December 2024, the **Northern Territory had the highest ARF/RHD prevalence among all Australians, 1,072 per 100,000**. Among First Nations people, the NT also recorded the greatest number and highest rate of new RHD diagnoses in 2024: **141 diagnoses, 180.2 per 100,000**. [AIHW, Acute rheumatic fever and rheumatic heart disease in Australia — summary](https://www.aihw.gov.au/reports/indigenous-australians/arf-rhd/contents/summary)
- AIHW explicitly says poor living conditions and household overcrowding increase exposure to group A streptococcus, which can lead to ARF and RHD; however, the slide should present this as the **why**, not claim that a bed itself prevents RHD. [AIHW, Acute rheumatic fever and rheumatic heart disease in Australia 2024](https://www.aihw.gov.au/getmedia/c0e7c3d8-f9c1-40fa-b8d3-e2a08a90b935/aihw-cvd-100-acute-rheumatic-fever-rheumatic-heart-disease-australia-2024.pdf?inline=true)

**Short card option:** `51.3% / of First Nations households in very remote NT needed one or more extra bedrooms.` Add beneath, smaller: `The NT has Australia's highest recorded ARF/RHD prevalence.`

## 2. COMMUNITY ENTERPRISE — Too little production is owned locally

**Hero data:** **3.1%**

**Slide copy:** Only 3.1% of employed First Nations people in the NT managed their own business in 2021—the lowest share of any state or territory.

**Evidence and limits**

- ABS counted **382** First Nations owner-managers in the NT in the 2021 Census. They were **3.1% of employed First Nations people aged 15+**, versus 6.9% nationally; the NT share was the lowest jurisdictional rate. “Managed their own business” includes owner-managers of incorporated and unincorporated enterprises, with or without employees. [ABS, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people who managed their own business in 2021](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-people-who-managed-their-own-business-2021)
- This is a **proxy for limited local enterprise participation**, not a direct measure of community ownership, community-led production, manufacturing capacity, or businesses located in remote communities. No official statistic found measures “the lack of community-led production” directly.
- Official policy evidence supports the structural problem without supplying a production count: NIAA states that remote Indigenous businesses face barriers including low population density, distance from expertise and markets, limited infrastructure, and access to suitable advice. [NIAA, Indigenous Business Sector Strategy](https://www.niaa.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/publications/ibss_strategy.pdf)

**Short card option:** `3.1% / of employed First Nations people in the NT managed their own business.` Caption it `A proxy for how little enterprise is locally owned—not a direct production measure.`

## 3. EMPLOYMENT — Opportunity falls sharply with remoteness

**Hero data:** **38.1%**

**Slide copy:** In 2022–23, only 38.1% of First Nations people aged 15–64 in very remote Australia were employed, compared with 68.0% in major cities.

**Evidence and definitions**

- AIHW analysis of the ABS 2022–23 National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Survey found employment declined consistently with remoteness: **68.0% in major cities, 43.2% in remote areas and 38.1% in very remote areas**. In very remote areas, 49.1% were not in the labour force and 12.8% were unemployed. Geography is Australia-wide; population is First Nations people aged 15–64. [AIHW, Employment of First Nations people](https://www.aihw.gov.au/reports/australias-welfare/indigenous-employment)
- For a Census/Closing the Gap measure, **35.0%** of First Nations people aged 25–64 in very remote Australia were employed in 2021, compared with 62.1% in major cities. The very-remote rate was the only remoteness category to decline from 2016. [Productivity Commission, Closing the Gap outcome 8](https://www.pc.gov.au/closing-the-gap-data/dashboard/outcome-area/economic-participation/)
- Do not mix the 38.1% and 35.0% figures without their age groups and sources: they use different collections and denominators.

**Short card option:** `38.1% / First Nations employment in very remote Australia—versus 68% in major cities.`

## 4. WASTE — Most NT waste is still landfilled

**Hero data:** **275,190t**

**Slide copy:** The NT processed 487,580 tonnes of waste in 2023–24. More than half—275,190 tonnes—went to landfill.

**Evidence and definitions**

- The NT Government-commissioned material flow analysis reports **487,580 tonnes of NT waste processed in 2023–24**: **275,190 tonnes (56.4%) went to landfill**, 99,600 tonnes (20.4%) were reused, 61,930 tonnes (12.7%) recycled, and 50,865 tonnes (10.4%) stockpiled or transported elsewhere. [NT Department of Trade, Business and Asian Relations, *The Economic Contribution of the Waste and Recycling Industry to the Northern Territory Economy 2023–24*, p. 15](https://dtbar.nt.gov.au/media/docs/industries/waste-and-recycling/wrint-ec-report-nov-2024.pdf)
- Of the recycled stream, **4,933 tonnes was plastic**. Only **15,597 tonnes (25.2%) of all recycled material stayed in the NT**; the remainder went interstate or overseas. The report estimates each tonne recycled in the NT creates $465 in net economic activity and every 431 tonnes supports one job.
- “Processed” is the report's flow measure; it should not be relabelled “waste generated.” The figures cover the whole NT, not remote communities specifically.

**Short card option:** `275,190t / of NT waste went to landfill in 2023–24—56.4% of all waste processed.`

## Suggested four-card rhythm

1. **HEALTH — 51.3%**  
   First Nations households in very remote NT needing at least one extra bedroom.  
   *The NT also carries Australia's highest recorded ARF/RHD prevalence.*

2. **COMMUNITY ENTERPRISE — 3.1%**  
   Of employed First Nations people in the NT managed their own business.  
   *Proxy only: official data does not isolate community-led production.*

3. **EMPLOYMENT — 38.1%**  
   First Nations employment in very remote Australia, versus 68% in major cities.

4. **WASTE — 275,190t**  
   NT waste sent to landfill in 2023–24—56.4% of all waste processed.

## Narrative bridge

These are not four unrelated deficits. They reveal where value currently leaves community: overcrowded homes carry health risk; products are largely supplied rather than made locally; paid work thins as distance grows; and recoverable material is buried or exported. The next slide can turn each problem into one part of the Goods on Country model—durable household goods, community enterprise, local paid work and local material recovery—without claiming the bed alone solves housing or RHD.
