# Brian M. Davis support letters: two drafts

For the Goods on Country application, $99,750, 133 Stretch Beds, closing 25 September 2026.
Drafted 17 September. **Drafts for the signatories to edit, not to send as written.**

## What these letters have to do

They are **delivery partner letters**, not referees. Referees are explicitly not required
below $100,000 a year and the ask is $99,750.

Each letter carries five things, because each maps to something the application commits to:

| The letter says | Because the application commits to |
|---|---|
| We are a delivery partner and we have agreed | The four-communities-confirmed field, still open |
| We would hold a first stock of beds and sell them | Outcome 2 and 3: four holding, four selling |
| The money from those sales stays with us | The whole model |
| Young people are paid and we set the terms | Outcome 1: about 266 paid hours across 133 beds |
| What we have already done with Goods | Evidence rather than goodwill |

**Explicit about the role, indicative about the number, conditional on award.** Do not ask
either organisation to commit to a hard number on a hard date. The grant is not awarded, the
Board decides 19 November, and the application's own risk answer says each community confirms
before the project starts and stock moves elsewhere if one declines.

## Two cautions

**Kristy Bloomfield is a director of Goods on Country Ltd.** Her letter is a related-party
letter. Declared it is fine, undeclared it is a problem, particularly with Anita's governance
condition already on this application. The disclosure is written into the draft. The arm's
length alternative from the same organisation is Tanya Turner, Operations Manager.

**The Oonchiumpa draft has thin verified facts behind it.** Homeland's history is verifiable:
they paid for forty Stretch Beds and two washing machines in May 2026, pressed in Queensland
and assembled at Gamardi by local young workers. For Oonchiumpa the consortium relationship and
the Alice Springs and Utopia work are supportable, but nothing is verified about Oonchiumpa
itself running build days or buying beds. The gaps are marked. **No letter goes out with a
bracket still in it.**

## Letter 1: Homeland School Company

*[Homeland School Company letterhead]*

[Date]

The Grants Committee
Brian M. Davis Charitable Foundation

**Re: Goods on Country Ltd, application for 133 Stretch Beds**

I am the Chief Executive Officer of Homeland School Company. I am writing to support the
application Goods on Country has put to you, and to confirm that we are a delivery partner
for it.

We have already bought from Goods and paid for it. In May 2026 we paid for forty Stretch Beds
and two washing machines for Maningrida. The beds were pressed at the Goods facility in
Queensland, sent to us flat packed, and assembled here by young workers from the community at
Gamardi. I mention that because it is the difference between receiving furniture and receiving
work.

*[Nic: two or three sentences in your own words. Why you bought them, where they went, and what
you saw when the young people were building them. This is the part the Foundation will read
twice.]*

If the Foundation funds this, we would take a first stock of around 33 beds as our own trading
stock, sell them ourselves and keep the full sale price. We would confirm the final number with
Goods before the project starts. We decide who works, what hours they work and what they are
paid. Goods does not set that and does not take a share of what we sell. That is what makes
this worth our time rather than another program delivered to us.

Goods is early and we are not pretending otherwise. What I can tell you is that we paid them,
they delivered, and young people here were paid for the work.

Yours sincerely,

**Nic Sharah**
Chief Executive Officer
Homeland School Company
